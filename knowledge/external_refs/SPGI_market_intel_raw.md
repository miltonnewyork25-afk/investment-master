## 3. Rating Agency Regulatory Environment (2025-2026)

### SEC Actions (US)
- **Sep 2024**: SEC charged six credit rating agencies with recordkeeping failures
  - S&P Global Ratings: **$20M civil penalty** (off-channel communications — WhatsApp, personal texts)
  - Moody's: $20M penalty
  - Fitch: $8M penalty
  - Required to retain compliance consultants
- No major new SEC rulemaking for NRSROs identified in 2025-2026

### ESMA / EU Actions
- **ESMA fine**: S&P Global Ratings Europe fined **EUR 1.11M** for premature publication of credit ratings (before securities issued/announced) + failures in discontinuing ratings
- **EU Green Bond Regulation**: S&P authorized as external reviewer under Regulation (EU) 2023/2631; transition period ends June 21, 2026
- **EU ESG Ratings Regulation**: Entered into force Jan 2, 2025; applies from **July 2, 2026**; ESMA will supervise; providers must obtain authorization by Nov 2026
- ESMA tightening guidelines on "private" ratings — even non-public assessments must meet transparency standards

### Regulatory Landscape Assessment
- Current environment is **enforcement-oriented** (recordkeeping, process compliance) rather than structural reform
- No new DOJ antitrust actions identified against rating agencies
- ESG ratings regulation is the **biggest new regulatory frontier** — may create compliance costs but also barriers to entry
- The oligopoly structure (S&P + Moody's + Fitch = 91% of revenue) remains intact and unchallenged

## 4. MCO vs SPGI Competitive Dynamics

### Market Share
- **Big Two**: S&P + Moody's collectively ~80% of global ratings market (~40% each)
- **Big Three** with Fitch: ~91% of total NRSRO revenue
- No meaningful share shifts observed in 2024-2025

### Revenue Growth Comparison
| Metric | SPGI | MCO |
|---|---|---|
| 5-year revenue CAGR (2019-2024) | 16.2% ($7B→$14B) | 8.0% ($5B→$7B) |
| 2024 revenue growth | 13.7% | 19.8% |
| FY2025 revenue | $15.3B | ~$7.5B (est.) |

### Strategic Divergence
- **SPGI**: Diversified data conglomerate (Ratings=31% of revenue); acquiring private markets data; spinning off Mobility
- **MCO**: More concentrated on credit/risk analytics; investing in Moody's Analytics platform; targeting 50% margins in 2025
- **MCO advantage**: "Purer" credit cycle play — outperforms in strong issuance years (e.g., 2024: +19.8% vs SPGI's +13.7%)
- **SPGI advantage**: Diversification smooths cyclicality; Indices/Market Intelligence provide recurring revenue ballast

### Private Credit Battleground
- Both expanding aggressively into private credit ratings and valuations
- SPGI's private credit division saw double-digit growth in 2025
- This is the key competitive frontier as private credit market expands

## 5. AI Impact on SPGI

### AI as Opportunity (Management's Narrative)
- **CreditCompanion (May 2025)**: GenAI tool on RatingsDirect/Capital IQ Pro platform — NLP + LLM + RAG for credit risk research
- **Kensho AI**: Clients can query trillions of data points via natural language across entire S&P ecosystem
- **Google Cloud partnership (late 2025)**: Integrating Gemini Enterprise models into products
- **"Agentic AI" focus**: Published "Big Picture 2026 AI Outlook: Unleashing Agentic Potential" — positioning as thought leader

### AI as Threat
- Goldman Sachs cited **AI disruption concerns** when lowering Moody's price target after SPGI guidance
- Market interpreted 2026 guidance miss partly through AI lens — higher investment costs + potential disruption to traditional data/analytics moats
- Key risk: If AI commoditizes basic credit analysis, it could pressure **Market Intelligence** subscription revenues (37% of total)

### AI as Revenue Driver
- Tech/AI issuance hit **16.7% of global nonfinancial bonds** in 2025 (vs much lower historically)
- AI infrastructure buildout driving increased debt issuance → more rating fees
- AI companies becoming major Ratings customers

### Assessment
- Near-term: AI is a **net positive** (new products, efficiency gains, increased issuance from AI companies)
- Medium-term risk: Commoditization of basic data/analytics could pressure Market Intelligence
- SPGI's moat in Ratings and Indices is AI-resistant (regulatory mandate + network effects); Market Intelligence is more exposed

### Portfolio Actions
- **Divesting**: Mobility (spinoff), OSTTRA (sold), EDM, thinkFolio
- **Acquiring**: With Intelligence ($1.8B, private markets data)
- **Net effect**: Concentrating on higher-margin, higher-moat financial data core

## 8. Pricing Power Signals

### Ratings Fee Increases (Direct Evidence)
- **Jan 2025 list price**: Corporate bond rating fee increased to **8.10 bps** (from 7.90 bps in 2024) = **+2.5% YoY**
- **Minimum transaction fee**: Raised to **$145,000** (from $140,000 in 2024) = **+3.6% YoY**
- Morningstar assessment: "Continued healthy pricing power reflecting the ratings segment's network effect and wide moat"
- Pattern: Consistent annual price increases, typically 2-4%

### Index Licensing (Indirect Evidence)
- ETF AUM tied to SPDJI indices exceeded **$4 trillion** in Q1 2025
- Asset-linked fees scale automatically with market appreciation — "passive pricing power"
- Indices segment growing 10-12% (2026 guide) with 69% margins — pricing power intact

### Data Subscriptions (Market Intelligence)
- S&P Capital IQ Pro subscription pricing not publicly disclosed in detail
- Market Intelligence growing 5.5-7% organically — suggests modest pricing + volume growth
- High switching costs (embedded in client workflows) support pricing power
- Vendr data suggests enterprise contracts range widely ($50K-$500K+)

### Overall Pricing Power Assessment
- **Ratings**: Strong — regulatory mandate + duopoly = consistent above-inflation pricing
- **Indices**: Very strong — asset-linked fees = automatic price escalator; switching costs enormous
- **Energy/Platts**: Strong — Platts benchmarks are industry standard for commodity pricing
- **Market Intelligence**: Moderate — more competitive; AI could pressure over time
- **Mobility**: Moderate — CARFAX has consumer brand moat but segment being spun off

## Key Takeaways for Deep Dive

1. **Valuation reset**: Trading at ~21x forward (vs 30x 5-year avg) after guidance disappointment — rare entry point or value trap?
2. **Portfolio simplification**: Mobility spinoff + divestitures + With Intelligence acquisition = more focused, higher-margin SPGI
3. **IHS Markit integration**: Success story — 50.4% margins prove cost synergies delivered; revenue synergies harder to isolate but cross-selling growing
4. **AI dual-edged**: Products like CreditCompanion are bullish; but Goldman flagging AI disruption risk suggests market uncertainty on net impact
5. **Regulatory moat intact**: No structural reform threatening the Big Two oligopoly; ESG regulation creates new opportunity + barrier
6. **Pricing power verified**: 2.5% annual rating fee increases + asset-linked index fees = durable pricing power across core segments
7. **Competitive position vs MCO**: SPGI is more diversified but lower-margin overall; MCO is purer credit play with higher cyclicality
8. **Smart money divided**: Some major additions (T. Rowe, Rockefeller) but also large exits (UBS, Lombard Odier) — no consensus post-guidance miss
