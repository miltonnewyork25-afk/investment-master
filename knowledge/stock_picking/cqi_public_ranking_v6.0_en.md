## What We Found

### Finding 1: Regulatory moats run deeper than technology moats

Three of the top four companies are **institutionally embedded** — CPRT (quasi-institutionalized insurance salvage channel), SPGI and FICO (regulators literally require their products). Moody's shares the same moat as S&P Global.

What they have in common: **even if a competitor built something better, regulation and industry practice would still force customers to use the incumbent.** That is the deepest kind of moat — rivals are not beaten by technology, they are excluded by institutions.

### Finding 2: The best businesses do not always make the best compounders

|---------|:--------------:|:--------:|:-----------------:|:----------:|:---:|
| TSMC | 53.5 | #1 | x0.65 | #21 | -20 |
| NVIDIA | 52.0 | #3 | x0.70 | #17 | -14 |
| S&P Global | 50.8 | #4 | x1.00 | #2 | +2 |
| Amazon | 50.0 | #5 | x0.82 | #10 | -5 |
| FICO | 49.5 | #7 | x1.00 | #3 | +4 |

CQI does not measure "how good is this business." It measures "will this business still be this good in ten years." TSMC and NVIDIA are flawless on quality, but geopolitics and CapEx cycles pull them off the pedestal. S&P Global and FICO, immune to recessions, sit quietly near the top.

### Finding 3: Big Tech moats come from entirely different sources

All five have deep moats, but from completely different sources. Apple is the only tech giant with a physical moat (custom silicon + supply chain), but also the only one that does not monetize data — a privacy brand advantage in a pre-AI world, a fuel shortage in an AI world.

### Finding 4: Two kinds of "getting stronger" — and they are not the same

The v5.0 upgrade: distinguishing **Structural Widening** from **Execution Strengthening**. Both mean the moat is deepening. But the nature is fundamentally different.

**Structural Widening ↑** — Industry structure, physics, or regulation is making the moat deeper **regardless of what management does**. Even with a bad CEO, the trend holds. This is the strongest positive signal.

**Execution Strengthening ↗** — The company is **actively** deepening its moat through market share gains, ecosystem expansion, or product innovation. This depends on management. If execution falters, the trend can reverse.

| Trend | Count | Key Names | What Is Driving It | What It Means |
|:-----:|:-----:|-----------|-------------------|--------------|
| ↑ Widening | 3 | ETN, ANET, KLAC | Physics and structural forces — electrification, AI infrastructure, advanced node inspection | Time is on your side. Management is not the key variable |
| ↗ Strengthening | 8 | CPRT, COST, AMZN, GOOGL, PLTR, VRT, HLT, RCL | Market share expansion, ecosystem extension, product launches | Keep watching management execution. Trend is reversible |
| → Stable | 19 | SPGI, MCO, MSFT, ASML, META... | Moat intact, no material change | Current CQI is a reliable long-term reference |
| ↘ Softening | 4 | AAPL, Visa, PG, LRCX | Regulation, substitution, or competition structurally eroding | Slow but irreversible. Watch for acceleration |
| ↓ Eroding | 4 | FICO, ARM, SBUX, SMCI | Institutions loosening, open-source advancing, brand damaged, margins compressing | Current CQI may overstate future quality |
| ⇊ Collapsing | 1 | INTC | Losing share in every market at once | The moat is nearly gone |

**The difference between ↑ and ↗**:

| Dimension | ↑ Structural Widening | ↗ Execution Strengthening |
|-----------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| **Driver** | Industry structure / physics / regulation | Management decisions / execution |
| **Reversibility** | Irreversible (unless industry changes) | Reversible (execution drops = trend reverses) |
| **CEO dependence** | Low — trend holds regardless | High — CEO is the key variable |
| **Typical signal** | TAM expanding on its own / share rising passively | Active share capture / new product launches |

**Case study**:
- **KLAC ↑**: As chips shrink from 7nm → 3nm → 2nm, inspection steps rise from 100 → 200 → 300+. This is **driven by physics** — more advanced nodes have more defects and need more inspection. KLAC does not need to do anything for its WFE share to keep rising.
- **GOOGL ↗**: Gemini is rolling out across Search, Cloud, Android, and YouTube. The moat is deepening, but this depends on **continuous investment and product execution**. If Gemini falls behind GPT, the trend could reverse.

**The most alarming signal on this list**: FICO ranks #3 but its moat trend is ↓ Eroding. The FHFA approved VantageScore in 2022 — that was the inflection point. **CQI 72 reflects the moat that was. If the trend holds, CQI could fall below 60 within three to five years.**

**The most interesting signal**: Only three companies are ↑ Structurally Widening (ETN, ANET, KLAC). Their moats are being deepened by industry structure and physics — forces that are nearly impossible to reverse.

> Hemingway wrote about bankruptcy: "Two ways. Gradually, then suddenly." Moat erosion works the same way. Strengthening is gradual (+0.1 per year). Erosion can be nonlinear — slow, slow, slow, then all at once.

### Finding 5: The ratings duopoly is a sleeping pricing-power giant

S&P Global and Moody's both sit at pricing power Stage 1.0–1.5 (barely tapped), while FICO has already reached Stage 2.3 (approaching the ceiling). What this means:
- **SPGI/MCO**: Operating margins have 10–15 percentage points of expansion ahead (42%/45% today vs. theoretical ceiling of 55–60%)
- **FICO**: Operating margins have already run from 20% to 47%, but regulatory backlash and VantageScore are closing in on the pricing ceiling

Untapped pricing power = more room to run. But FICO's story is also a warning: **the most dangerous moment for an institutional monopoly is when it exercises pricing power loudly enough to attract political attention.**

## How We Score

The **Compounding Quality Index (CQI)** evaluates long-term business quality across six dimensions:

| Dimension | What It Measures | The Core Question |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Pricing Power** | Ability to raise prices above inflation without losing customers | If you raised prices 10%, how many customers would leave? |
| **Customer Stickiness** | Lock-in depth x revenue recurrence | How much time and money does it take a customer to switch? |
| **Moat Depth** | Institutional embedding + network effects + ecosystem lock-in | How many years would it take a competitor to catch up? |
| **Growth Runway** | Revenue engine clarity x remaining TAM | Can this business double its revenue over the next decade? |
| **Capital Discipline** | Margin elasticity + capital allocation efficiency | Is management spending the cash wisely? |
| **Competitive Barriers** | Data flywheel + scale economics + physical moat | How much would it cost a new entrant to replicate this business? |

- CQI is scored out of 100, built on 42 quantitative sub-factors, using 11 years of data (2014–2025)
- 39 companies rated + 12 candidates on the watchlist = 51 companies covered. Candidates will receive official scores after deep dive completion
- **Moat Trend** is the dynamic layer on top of the static score:

| Symbol | Meaning | Driver Nature | How to Read It |
|:------:|---------|:------------:|---------------|
| ↑ Widening | Industry structure or physics irreversibly deepening the moat | Management-independent | Time is on your side. Current CQI may understate future quality |
| ↗ Strengthening | Company actively deepening the moat through execution | Execution-dependent | Watch management. Trend is reversible if execution falters |
| → Stable | Moat intact, no material change | — | Current CQI is a reliable long-term reference |
| ↘ Softening | Regulation, substitution, or competition structurally eroding | Hard to reverse | Slow but directionally irreversible |
| ↓ Eroding | The moat is being actively opened by specific threats | Identifiable attacker | Current CQI may overstate future quality |
| ⇊ Collapsing | Moat evaporating across multiple fronts | Multiple forces | CQI could drop sharply over the next 3–5 years |

- **A high CQI is not a buy signal. A low CQI is not a sell signal.** CQI evaluates business model quality, not stock price
- Trend judgments are grounded in data from our deep-dive reports. Companies marked "Coming Soon" carry preliminary trend assessments subject to revision
