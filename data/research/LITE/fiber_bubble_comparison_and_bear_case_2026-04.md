### 1.5 What Caused the 2000 Fiber Bubble Burst?

**Root Causes (in order of importance):**

1. **Fabricated Demand Forecasts**: WorldCom claimed internet traffic was doubling every 100 days -- actual rate was doubling annually. Companies built capacity for 100x the real demand trajectory.

2. **Catastrophic Overcapacity**: Telecom companies invested >$500B (mostly debt-financed) into fiber. By the bust, **85-95% of installed fiber remained "dark" (unused)** even 4 years later. Less than 5% was ever lit.

3. **Technology Multiplier Reduced Demand**: Wavelength-division multiplexing (WDM) increased single-fiber capacity by **100x**, reducing need for new fiber. Data capacity per fiber was doubling every 9 months (per Lucent).

4. **Debt-Fueled Overbuilding**: Telecom Act of 1996 created dozens of CLECs (competitive local exchange carriers), each building "fiber to corner the market." All building for the same customers.

5. **Accounting Fraud**: WorldCom ($3.8B fraud), Nortel ($3B improperly booked), Global Crossing, Adelphia -- multiple companies inflating revenue to justify continued investment.

6. **Telecom CapEx Peaked at ~$120B/year** (2000 dollars), one of the largest infrastructure build-outs in history.

**Result**: Between 2000-2002, global telecom stocks lost >$2 trillion in market value. WorldCom, Global Crossing, 360networks -- multiple bankruptcies.

### 1.6 Current AI Optical Build: Overcapacity Evidence?

**Investment Scale:**
- Hyperscaler CapEx (Big 5): **>$600B in 2026**, +36% YoY from 2025
- ~75% ($450B) directly tied to AI infrastructure
- 2025 data center CapEx jumped **57% YoY** (Dell'Oro Group)
- Bain & Co: AI compute demand growth >2x Moore's Law, would require ~$500B annual investment

**Investment-to-Revenue Ratio (Key Bubble Indicator):**
- Combined 2025 AI cloud revenue: **~$50-60B**
- Combined 2025 AI-related CapEx: **>$380B**
- **Ratio: 6:1 investment-to-revenue** -- requires years of triple-digit growth to justify
- Compare: 2000 telecom CapEx peak ~$120B on ~$300B telecom revenue (0.4:1)
- **Current AI ratio is ~15x worse than the 2000 telecom bubble's ratio**

**Overcapacity Signals:**
- MIT study: **95% of AI pilot projects fail** to yield meaningful results despite >$40B in generative AI investment
- Bain: Even most aggressive enterprise AI take-up would only generate $1.2T revenue vs $2T needed -- **$800B spending gap**
- AWS claims capacity "consumed as fast as we put it in" -- but this is what every bubble builder says

**Key Differences from 2000:**
1. Buyers are 5-7 mega-cap companies (not hundreds of CLECs) -- more concentrated, more rational
2. AI companies are generating real revenue on real business models (unlike most dot-coms)
3. Data centers require continuous electricity/cooling -- stranded assets are costlier than dark fiber
4. No widespread accounting fraud (so far)

## Part 2: Bear Case / Short Thesis for LITE

### 2.1 Short Interest: 21.9% -- Why So High?

**Current Data:**
- Short interest: **11.49M shares** (up from 10.90M in prior reporting period)
- Short % of float: **21.89%**
- This is among the highest in the semiconductor/optical sector

**Main Bear Arguments (synthesized from multiple sources):**

1. **Valuation Extreme**: PE 238x (TTM), stock up 1,600% from 52-week low of $45.65 to high of $827.56
2. **Cyclicality Denial**: Stock priced for structural growth, but optical components are historically among the most cyclical tech subsectors
3. **Customer Concentration Risk**: Heavy dependence on hyperscaler AI CapEx spend, which could slow or reverse
4. **Fixed Cost Structure**: Manufacturing costs are highly fixed -- revenue decline hits EBITDA margins disproportionately
5. **Weak Industrial Tech Segment**: Non-AI segments (telecom, industrial) remain weak with inventory adjustments
6. **FCF Negative Despite Revenue Explosion**: FY2025 Owner FCF -$282M despite rapid revenue growth

### 2.2 Published Short Seller Theses

**No Kerrisdale Capital short report found** on LITE specifically. However, bear narratives from multiple sources include:

- **Coherent (II-VI) Competition**: Ramping 6-inch InP wafers yielding 4x more dies at lower cost
- **Silicon Photonics (SiPho) Risk**: Tower and GlobalFoundries are **5x-ing SiPho capacity**, which could "route around" EML-based transceivers entirely
- **Startup Disruption**: Mesh Optical Technologies (SpaceX alumni, $50M raised) building 1.6T transceivers using semiconductor-style packaging
- **Vertical Integration Risk**: If NVIDIA or Broadcom internalize optical supply chains or push CPO faster, Lumentum's "laser moat" is challenged
- **Copper Persistence**: Industry preference for copper interconnects in scale-up applications could delay optical adoption timeline

### 2.3 Convertible Bond Arbitrage and Short Interest

**Key Convert Details:**
- **$1.265B** in 0.375% Convertible Senior Notes due **2032** (issued Sep 2025, including $165M overallotment)
- Conversion price: **$187.77/share** (40% premium over $134.12 at pricing)
- Conversion rate: 5.3257 shares per $1,000 principal
- **Capped call transactions** with Citibank, Goldman Sachs et al. -- cap price **$268.24** (100% premium)
- Prior 0.50% Convertible Notes due 2026: **$581.1M repurchased** using proceeds
- Full conversion could add **~4.2% shares** to outstanding

**Convert Arbitrage Impact on Short Interest:**
- Classic convert arb: buy the convert bond + short the stock to delta-hedge
- With $1.265B in converts outstanding, the delta-hedge short position could be substantial
- At conversion price $187.77, full conversion = ~6.7M shares. Delta-hedge typically ~50-70% of conversion shares = **3.4-4.7M shares shorted** for hedging
- Total short: 11.49M shares. Convert-related hedging could explain **30-40% of short interest**
- **Implication**: Actual directional short interest may be closer to 12-14% of float, not 21.9%

**Capped Call Complexity:**
- Capped calls limit dilution between $187.77 (conversion) and $268.24 (cap)
- At current stock ~$700+, capped calls are deep in-the-money -- counterparties (banks) are delta-hedging by buying stock
- This creates **positive gamma** for the stock -- price momentum is self-reinforcing
- **Risk**: If stock drops toward $268 cap, counterparty hedging reverses -- creates **downward pressure**

### 2.4 AI CapEx Cycle Peak: Historical Precedents

**Semiconductor CapEx Cycle Peak-to-Trough Declines:**

| Cycle | Peak-to-Trough Decline | Duration | Trigger |
|-------|----------------------|----------|---------|
| **2001 (Dot-Com)** | **-32% (market), -60-70% for optical** | 2-3 years | Telecom overbuild + fraud |
| **2008-2009 (GFC)** | **-40% YoY** (semi CapEx), **-60-70%** for worst-hit | 1.5 years | Financial crisis |
| **2018-2019** | **-12%** (semi market) | 1 year | Trade war + inventory |
| **2020 (COVID)** | **-30%+** brief | 6 months | Demand shock (brief) |

**Optical Components Specifically (2001-2003):**
- JDS Uniphase: Revenue -67% (peak quarter to trough quarter)
- Ciena: Revenue **-81%** (peak annual to trough annual)
- Corning fiber revenue: **-60-80%**
- 10G transceiver prices: **>$1,000 (2001) to <$100 (2010)** -- 90%+ price erosion over decade

**Key Implication for LITE**: If AI CapEx peaks in FY2028 and declines even modestly (30%), optical component revenue could fall 40-60% given operating leverage and inventory effects.

### 2.5 FY2029 Consensus Revenue Drop of 37% -- What's the Mechanism?

**The Consensus Numbers (from P0.75 crystallization):**
- FY2028 consensus revenue: **$6.6B**
- FY2029 consensus revenue: **$4.2B**
- Decline: **-$2.4B (-37%)**

**Probable Mechanisms:**

1. **AI CapEx Digestion Cycle**: After 3-4 years of >30% annual CapEx growth, hyperscalers enter a "digestion" period. Historical precedent: cloud CapEx flattened in 2019 after 2016-2018 ramp, causing optical stocks to drop 30-50%.

2. **800G-to-1.6T Transition Gap**: Each generational transition (100G→400G→800G→1.6T) creates a period where:
   - Old product (800G) volume declines as ASPs fall
   - New product (1.6T) hasn't ramped to full volume
   - The gap creates a revenue "valley"
   - LITE's current revenue boom is largely 800G. By FY2028-2029, 800G will be commoditized

3. **Inventory Digestion**: Hyperscalers over-order during supply-constrained periods (current "under shipping" by 25-30%), then reduce orders once supply normalizes. This is the classic semiconductor inventory bullwhip effect.

4. **Base Effect**: $6.6B is an extremely high base. Even flat demand = no growth. Any pause = revenue decline.

5. **Price Erosion**: Optical transceiver ASPs historically decline 15-25% annually in mature products. 800G ASPs will compress, and 1.6T may not fully offset.

6. **Competition Catches Up**: By FY2029, Coherent's 6-inch InP, SiPho alternatives, and potential customer vertical integration (Meta, Google self-sourcing) could erode market share.

**Historical Parallel**: Lumentum itself experienced this pattern in FY2022-2024. Revenue went from $1.77B (FY2022) to $1.36B (FY2024) -- a 23% decline as the telecom/datacom cycle turned down.
