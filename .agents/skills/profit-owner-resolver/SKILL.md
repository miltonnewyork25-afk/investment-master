# Profit Owner Resolver

## What this skill does
This skill identifies who really gets paid.

Many companies are exposed to a trend.
Few actually own the economics.

This skill exists to separate:
- revenue exposure
from
- profit ownership

It also distinguishes between:

- **Immediate owner**
  The company that benefits first and most directly from the current wave.

- **Bridge owner**
  A company whose economics are supported by more than one linked demand chain or thematic bucket.

- **Duration owner**
  A company whose economics persist longer than the market expects through installed base, services, replacement, maintenance, certification, or long-tail monetization.

The goal is not just to identify who wins first.
The goal is to identify who wins best and who wins longest.

## Default workflow
1. Identify all obvious beneficiaries.
2. Ask who books the revenue.
3. Ask who keeps the gross profit.
4. Ask who converts the opportunity into cash flow.
5. Ask who improves return on capital.
6. Check whether growth is capital-light or capital-hungry.
7. Check whether the economics are durable or temporary.
8. Ask whether a less obvious bridge owner exists.
9. Ask whether a less obvious duration owner exists.

## Ownership layers to examine

### 1. Revenue owner
Who books the immediate revenue?

### 2. Gross profit owner
Who captures the attractive margin?

### 3. Cash flow owner
Who turns the operating benefit into cash rather than reinvestment drag?

### 4. Return owner
Who improves return on capital, not just scale?

### 5. Bridge owner
Who benefits because they connect multiple real demand chains?

### 6. Duration owner
Who keeps getting paid after the first wave?
These often include installed-base service owners, certified aftermarket owners, replacement-cycle owners, consumables owners, and long-duration contract owners.

## What to ask

### Immediate owner questions
- Who gets paid first?
- Who gets the first visible revenue lift?
- Who is most directly tied to current demand?

### Bridge owner questions
- Does this company sit at the junction of multiple real demand chains?
- Would it still win if one narrative cooled down?
- Is the market misclassifying it into only one bucket?

#### Hard test (v2.1, from Quanta validation)

Passing "bridge owner" requires **both**:

1. **Trigger-mechanism independence** — the demand chains must be driven by *different forcing mechanisms* (e.g., asset aging + hyperscaler CapEx + policy mandate + industrial policy). Chains that share the same terminal demand (e.g., "more electricity") can still qualify *if* their trigger mechanisms are independent.
2. **Resilience threshold** — if the dominant chain collapses, at least one other chain must independently sustain ≥30% of current revenue or economics.

Chains that are merely "two expressions of the same driver" fail the test. What matters is whether collapse in one driver leaves meaningful economics intact.

### Duration owner questions
- Does this company monetize what gets installed after the market stops focusing on initial deployment?
- Does it benefit from service, maintenance, replacement, software, certification, parts, or lifecycle economics?
- Is the market underestimating how long this profit stream lasts?

## What good use looks like
A good output should clarify:
- who gets attention
- who gets revenue
- who gets margin
- who gets cash
- who gets true value creation
- who is the immediate owner
- who is the bridge owner
- who is the duration owner

Sometimes these are the same company.
Often they are not.

## Key reminder
A correct trend does not guarantee a correct stock.
Do not confuse participation with ownership.

### 2. Revenue is not enough
The best owner is not always the one with the fastest reported growth.

### 3. Immediate owner is not always the best investment
The market often sees the immediate owner first.
It may underappreciate the bridge owner or duration owner.

### 4. Duration matters
Longer-lived economics often deserve more attention than the first obvious revenue spike.

### 5. Bridge ownership matters
A company that links multiple demand systems may be more resilient and more mispriced than a single-theme leader.

## Flexibility
If ownership is split across several layers, say so.
Do not force a single winner if economics are genuinely mixed.

If the best answer is:
- near-term owner = one company
- long-duration owner = another company

say that explicitly.
