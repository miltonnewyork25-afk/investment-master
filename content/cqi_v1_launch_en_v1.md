**3/**

CPRT at #1 surprises people.

They auction wrecked cars. Doesn't sound exciting until you realize: two-thirds of U.S. accident vehicles go through their yards. Insurance companies have nowhere else to go.

200+ physical sites, most bought decades ago. Try getting the permits today.

---

**4/**

The one ranking our team argued about longest: FICO at #2, NVDA at #6.

NVDA's raw quality score is actually higher than FICO's.

But FICO gets paid every time someone checks a credit score. Millions of times a day. No purchase decision required.

NVDA gets paid when someone decides to spend $3B on a data center. Big difference.

---

**5/**

"NVDA at #6 — are you serious?"

Dead serious.

CUDA's moat is real. AI's growth runway is real. But NVDA's revenue sits on top of customer CapEx cycles. When the music slows, it doesn't slow gently.

We don't score how strong a company is right now. We score how stable it is in its worst year.

Two very different questions.

---

**6/**

FICO raised prices 7x in a decade. Zero customer churn.

Why? Because FICO scores are written into U.S. lending regulations. Banks don't choose to use FICO. They have to.

That's not pricing power. That's institutional lock-in.

---

**7/**

The one that breaks your heart: AVGO at #7.

Great acquirer. Strong moats in every niche it enters. But stock-based comp running near 12% of revenue is quietly diluting you while the headline numbers look clean.

---

**8/**

IHG at #10 doesn't mean it's a bad company.

Its real profit margin is around 65% — the reported numbers hide that behind accounting choices. But hospitality is cyclical. When recessions hit, the best business model in the world still loses bookings.

Quality ≠ value. A cheap stock can score low. An expensive stock can score high.
