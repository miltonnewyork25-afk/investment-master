# Prototype Library v1.0

Use prototypes as **starting anchors**, not cages.

The point is to reduce drift:
- what is this company structurally similar to?
- what score range would be surprising?
- which mistakes are most likely if you analyze it from the wrong prototype?

## P1 — Operational Control Layer

**Definition**
- Strong `I2` + `I3`
- Meaningful `I4`
- often eligible for `Stack Coherence Bonus`

**Typical signals**
- named operational layer
- governed actions
- auditability / dynamic permissions
- enterprise-critical workflow embedding

**Typical mistakes**
- over-scoring `I1` because the product feels “important”
- confusing tool usage with control layer ownership

---

## P2 — Split-Retain Execution Rail

**Definition**
- discovery or intent starts upstream
- monetization still happens when execution or completion flows back here

**Typical signals**
- strong `I3`
- strong `I8` across `Execution Routing` and `Transaction Completion`
- good `I1` if take-rate is meaningful

**Typical mistakes**
- over-scoring discovery ownership
- under-scoring execution depth because the upstream interface changed

---

## P3 — AI-Native Routing Layer

**Definition**
- owns the budget or task routing engine
- usually very strong `I3` and `I6`
- typically weak `I4/I7`

**Typical signals**
- single dominant routing stage
- algorithmic execution
- self-developed optimization stack
- strong economics, thin accountability

**Typical mistakes**
- mistaking high margin for full moat
- forgetting that thin authority makes the structure brittle

---

## P4 — Compliance-Backed Authority Owner

**Definition**
- the company does not just enable the workflow
- it meaningfully carries the outcome, exception, or compliance burden

**Typical signals**
- strong `I4`
- strong `I7`
- authority linked to monetization
- AI often helps throughput rather than replacing the business

**Typical mistakes**
- confusing governance tooling with real authority monetization

---

## P5 — Single-Point Physical Frozen

**Definition**
- a physical or engineering bottleneck that the budget must pass through

**Typical signals**
- capex lock
- physical execution lock
- hard replacement friction

**Typical mistakes**
- ignoring cyclicality because the moat is deep

---

## P6 — Pure Defensive Compliance

**Definition**
- one defensive moat matters
- expansion legs are weak or absent

**Typical signals**
- high compliance relevance
- low `E`
- often poor AI asymmetry

**Typical mistakes**
- mistaking protection for expansion

---

## P7 — Transition-Watch Expansion

**Definition**
- management is trying to move the value unit upward
- but the real economic engine has not moved with it

**Typical signals**
- obvious `PS > EE`
- AI narrative ahead of monetization
- sequencing strain

**Typical mistakes**
- scoring the story instead of the engine

---

## P10 — Duopoly-Scale Network

**Definition**
- discovery, distribution, and first-party signal are controlled by a very small number of scaled players

**Typical signals**
- discovery entry ownership
- first-party data scale
- multi-sided network effects

**Typical mistakes**
- underestimating how hard it is to dislodge distribution at this scale

---

## P11 — Legacy Workflow Platform Defending with AI

**Definition**
- the legacy workflow moat is still real
- discovery and lightweight creation/use cases are under AI pressure
- the company is using AI first to defend the franchise, not to build a brand-new control layer

**Typical signals**
- `I1` remains high-quality subscription/access
- `I6` remains strong
- `I8` shows discovery loss but execution/completion retention
- `I4/I7` are real but limited
- AI asymmetry is often `Neutral` or `Neutral Positive`

**Typical mistakes**
- calling it `Broad Alpha` because margins stay high
- calling it `Transition-Watch` because AI pressure is visible
- over-crediting AI features as new control points

**Boundary checks**
- vs `P6`: `P11` still has real expansion attempts
- vs `P7`: `P11` usually does **not** have severe `PS/EE` divergence
- vs `P2`: `P11` retains workflow depth, but not a comparably deep execution rail

## Reserved / Output Modifiers

These are useful labels, but not primary structural prototypes in `v1.0`:

- `Growth-Brittle`
- `Option-Stage`
- `Legacy-Decaying Front-End`

Use them as **output modifiers**, not first-pass prototype anchors.

---

## Boundary Triage: `P6` vs `P7` vs `P11`

Use this quick triage when a company feels defensive, pressured by AI, and awkward to classify.

| Question | If Yes | If No |
|---|---|---|
| Is `PS` clearly ahead of `EE`? | start from `P7` | continue |
| Is there almost no credible expansion leg? | start from `P6` | continue |
| Is the legacy workflow still strong, with discovery pressure but retained execution/completion? | start from `P11` | continue |
| Are margins still thick mainly because the old franchise remains intact? | points toward `P11` | may still be `P6` or `P7` |

### Fast read

- `P6`: the moat is mostly defensive and expansion is weak
- `P7`: the story moved upward before the engine did
- `P11`: the old workflow still matters, and AI is first being used to defend it

### Company shorthand

- `GTLB` is the clean `P6` anchor
- `CRM` is the clean `P7` anchor
- `ADBE` is the clean `P11` anchor

## How to Use the Library

### High confidence
- one prototype clearly fits
- use its range as a sanity check

### Medium confidence
- list top 2 prototypes
- treat the result as a blended structure

### Low confidence
- score independently
- if the pattern repeats across companies, consider proposing a new prototype
