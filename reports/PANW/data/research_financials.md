## 2. Billings Growth vs Revenue Growth Divergence

### Why Billings Matters (And Why PANW Is De-Emphasizing It)

**Definition**: Billings = Revenue + Change in Deferred Revenue (net of acquired deferred revenue)

Billings historically served as a leading indicator of demand for subscription/SaaS companies because it captures cash invoiced upfront for multi-year contracts. However, PANW management has explicitly de-emphasized billings in favor of RPO and NGS ARR as the primary demand indicators.

### Historical Billings vs Revenue ($B)

| Fiscal Year | Revenue | Billings (est.) | Rev Growth | Billings Growth | Gap |
|-------------|---------|-----------------|-----------|----------------|-----|
| FY2022 | $5.50 | ~$7.14 | +29.3% | ~28% | -1.3pp |
| FY2023 | $6.89 | ~$9.20 | +25.3% | ~29% | +3.7pp |
| FY2024 | $8.03 | ~$10.20 | +16.5% | ~11% | -5.5pp |
| FY2025 | $9.22 | ~$11.30* | +14.9% | ~11%* | -3.9pp |

*FY2025 billings estimated from deferred revenue change.

### Why the Divergence Matters for Investors

1. **FY2024 billings deceleration scared the market**: Billings growth dropped to ~11% while revenue grew 16.5%. The stock dropped ~25% on the FY2024 Q2 earnings report (Feb 2024) when billings guidance was cut

2. **Root cause — platformization strategy**: PANW offered free trials and deferred billing to consolidate customers onto its platform. This deliberately sacrificed near-term billings for long-term customer lock-in and higher lifetime value

3. **Payment terms shifting**: Increasing demand for annual (vs. multi-year upfront) payment terms on larger deals reduces billings without reducing committed revenue. The "cost of money" in a higher-rate environment made customers prefer annual billing

4. **RPO is the better metric now**: RPO captures total committed but unrecognized revenue regardless of billing schedule. RPO grew 24% YoY to $15.8B in Q4 FY2025 — far outpacing billings growth — showing demand strength that billings masked

5. **Deferred revenue dynamics**: Total deferred revenue grew from $6.99B (FY2022) to $9.30B (FY2023) to $11.48B (FY2024) to $12.75B (FY2025), a 82% increase over 3 years. This massive deferred revenue base provides revenue visibility for 2-3 years forward

3. **Long-term revenue conversion**: NGS ARR eventually converts to recognized subscription revenue over the contract term; the ~$5.9B ARR base provides a strong floor for future subscription revenue
4. **Platformization multiplier**: Customers adopting the platform (multiple NGS modules) have higher retention and expansion rates

## 5. Free Cash Flow Margin Analysis

### FCF Trend ($M)

| Fiscal Year | Revenue | Op CF | CapEx | FCF | FCF Margin | Adj FCF Margin* |
|-------------|---------|-------|-------|-----|-----------|----------------|
| FY2020 | $3,408 | $1,036 | $214 | $821 | 24.1% | ~28% |
| FY2021 | $4,256 | $1,503 | $116 | $1,387 | 32.6% | ~36% |
| FY2022 | $5,502 | $1,985 | $193 | $1,792 | 32.6% | ~36% |
| FY2023 | $6,893 | $2,778 | $146 | $2,631 | 38.2% | ~39% |
| FY2024 | $8,028 | $3,258 | $157 | $3,101 | 38.6% | ~40% |
| FY2025 | $9,222 | $3,716 | $246 | $3,470 | 37.6% | ~38% |

*Adjusted FCF margin per company reporting (excludes certain items like PP&E from acquisitions). FY2026 guidance: 38-39% adjusted FCF margin.

### Why PANW Has Exceptional FCF Margins (~38%)

**Factor 1: Massive Deferred Revenue Inflows (Primary Driver)**
- Customers pay upfront (or annually) for multi-year subscriptions, but revenue is recognized ratably over the contract term
- Total deferred revenue: $12.75B (FY2025), up from $5.02B in FY2021 — a 154% increase
- The annual *increase* in deferred revenue is essentially interest-free financing from customers
- In FY2025: Revenue was $9.22B but cash collected from customers was significantly higher (~$10.5B+ implied by deferred revenue growth + recognized revenue)

**Factor 2: Working Capital Tailwind**
- Working capital contributed $923M in FY2025 alone ($911M in FY2024, $551M in FY2023)
- Key dynamics:
  - **Accounts receivable**: Growing but slower than revenue (DSO stable ~146-152 days due to enterprise contract terms)
  - **Deferred revenue growth > revenue growth**: The gap between cash collected and revenue recognized creates persistent working capital surplus
  - **No inventory**: Pure software/subscription model has zero inventory carrying costs (unlike FTNT which has hardware inventory)
  - **Negative net working capital**: Current liabilities ($7.99B, mostly deferred revenue) exceed current assets ($7.10B)

**Factor 3: SBC as Non-Cash Operating Expense**
- $1.295B of SBC in FY2025 flows through the income statement but does NOT reduce operating cash flow
- This is the single largest add-back: SBC alone represents 14% of revenue that suppresses GAAP profitability but not cash generation
- Without SBC, GAAP operating income would be ~$2.54B (27.5% margin) vs. reported $1.24B (13.5% margin)

**Factor 4: Low Capital Intensity**
- CapEx/Revenue: 2.7% (FY2025), down from 6.3% (FY2020)
- PANW runs a capital-light model — cloud infrastructure costs are embedded in subscription COGS, not CapEx
- CapEx/Depreciation: 0.81x (FY2025) — investing slightly less than depreciation, suggesting infrastructure build-out is largely complete

**Factor 5: Deferred Payment Program**
- ~40% of FY2025 FCF came from deferred payments on deals signed in prior fiscal years
- $2B in scheduled deferred payments expected for FY2026
- This is essentially vendor financing that PANW extends to customers, collecting cash later but booking revenue ratably — when those payments arrive, they boost FCF

### Total Gross Margin Trend

| Fiscal Year | Revenue ($M) | COGS ($M) | Gross Margin |
|-------------|-------------|----------|-------------|
| FY2020 | $3,408 | $1,000 | 70.7% |
| FY2021 | $4,256 | $1,275 | 70.0% |
| FY2022 | $5,502 | $1,719 | 68.8% |
| FY2023 | $6,893 | $1,910 | 72.3% |

| FY2025 | $9,222 | $2,452 | 73.4% |
| Q1 FY2026 | $2,474 | $638 | 74.2% |

### Key Observations on Gross Margins

1. **Counter-intuitive finding**: Product gross margin (77-80%) is HIGHER than Subscription & Support gross margin (72-73%). This is unusual for software companies where subscription margins are typically higher.

2. **Why product margins are higher**: PANW's "product" includes high-margin software licenses bundled with hardware appliances. The increasing share of software form factors (44% of product revenue in Q1 FY2026) pushes product margins higher. True hardware-only margins would be much lower (~50-60%).

3. **Subscription margin compression pressure**: Cloud-delivered security (SASE, Prisma Cloud) requires significant infrastructure investment, creating COGS pressure. However, at scale, these should improve.

4. **Gross margin recovery**: After bottoming at 68.8% in FY2022 (hardware supply chain costs + rapid subscription infrastructure build), margins recovered to 73-74% in FY2024-FY2026 as scale economics kicked in.

5. **Non-GAAP gross margins are higher**: GAAP includes SBC in COGS. Non-GAAP gross margins are typically 2-3pp higher (~76-77%).

### Operating Leverage Summary

| Metric | FY2020 | FY2025 | Change | Direction |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|-----------|
| R&D/Revenue | 22.5% | 21.5% | -1.0pp | Modest leverage |
| S&M/Revenue | 44.6% | 33.6% | -11.0pp | **Strong leverage** |
| G&A/Revenue | 8.8% | 4.8% | -4.0pp | Strong leverage |
| Total OpEx/Rev | 75.9% | 59.9% | -16.0pp | **Strong leverage** |
| GAAP Op Margin | -5.3% | 13.5% | +18.8pp | **Massive improvement** |

### Key Operating Leverage Observations

1. **S&M is the biggest source of operating leverage**: Down 11pp over 5 years. The platformization strategy is working — customers consolidating onto PANW's platform require less sales effort per dollar of revenue. Cross-sell/up-sell within existing platform customers is cheaper than acquiring new logos.

2. **R&D relatively flat as % of revenue**: PANW continues to invest heavily in R&D ($1.98B in FY2025) to maintain competitive edge in AI-driven security, SASE, and XDR. The company is choosing to reinvest rather than harvest R&D leverage — this is intentional.

3. **G&A shows scale benefits**: Administrative costs growing much slower than revenue as back-office scales.

4. **Non-GAAP operating margins are much higher**: Removing SBC (~14% of revenue) shows non-GAAP operating margin of ~27-28% in FY2025. FY2026 guidance: 29.5-30.0% non-GAAP operating margin.

5. **Path to 30%+ non-GAAP operating margin**: Management has guided to continued margin expansion. The model shows: 74% gross margin - 15% R&D (ex-SBC) - 25% S&M (ex-SBC) - 4% G&A (ex-SBC) = ~30% non-GAAP operating margin.

| + SBC | 14.0% |
| + Amortization of acquired intangibles | ~1.5% |
