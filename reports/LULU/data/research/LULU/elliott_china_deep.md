### Key Pattern: Elliott's Consumer Playbook
1. **Identify operational underperformance** relative to brand strength
2. **Push for leadership change** (CEO replacement is the primary lever)
3. **Board representation** to enforce strategic accountability
4. **Operational focus**: portfolio simplification, margin improvement, SKU rationalization
5. **Timeline**: Typically 12-24 months to initial value creation; willing to hold 3-5 years for full thesis

### Success Rate Assessment
- **Board seat win rate**: Very high — secured seats at Phillips 66, Etsy, Match in H1 2024 alone
- **CEO change success**: Starbucks (Niccol), Lululemon (McDonald departure), B&N (Daunt installed) — near-100% on CEO change push

- **Criticism**: A 2024 CWA report argued Elliott's interventions impair long-term stock/bond returns and operating performance vs public market benchmarks, though this is contested

## 2. Barnes & Noble Turnaround — Elliott's Retail Masterclass

### Timeline
- **June 2019**: Elliott announces acquisition of B&N for $6.50/share ($683M total including debt assumption)
- **August 2019**: Acquisition completed
- **2019-2023**: James Daunt restructuring and store-level empowerment
- **2024**: Sale of publishing arm (Union Square & Co) to Hachette — focus on retail core
- **2025**: 67 new stores opened (highest in decades); plans for 60+ in 2026
- **H2 2026**: IPO planned for combined Waterstones + B&N entity; London preferred listing venue

### The Daunt Playbook (Most Relevant to LULU)
Elliott's single most important decision was hiring **James Daunt**, former CEO of UK bookseller Waterstones (which Elliott acquired in 2018). Daunt's approach:

1. **Decentralized merchandising**: Dismantled centralized "big-box" model; empowered local store managers to curate inventory
2. **Community hub transformation**: Stores became community gathering spaces, not just retail boxes
3. **Store quality over quantity**: Invested in existing stores before expanding
4. **Publishing divestiture**: Sold non-core publishing arm to focus exclusively on retail strengths
5. **Aggressive re-expansion**: After stabilization, opened 30 stores (2023), 50 (2024), 67 (2025)

### Financial Results
- **Revenue**: ~$3B annual sales (combined Waterstones + B&N)
- **Profit**: ~$400M annual profit by end of 2025
- **ROI**: $683M acquisition → multi-billion IPO valuation = estimated 5-8x+ return in ~7 years

### Relevance to LULU
- **Parallel**: Both B&N and LULU had strong brand equity but operational/strategic drift
- **Key difference**: B&N was a full acquisition (Elliott had total control); LULU is a minority activist position (~$1B of ~$30B+ market cap)
- **Daunt analog**: Elliott's push for Jane Nielsen as CEO mirrors the Daunt playbook — find an operator who can revitalize from within
- **Risk**: LULU's problems (Americas deceleration, no permanent CEO, founder proxy fight) are less existential than B&N's were (imminent category destruction by Amazon)

## 4. China Segment Profitability vs North America

### Segment Operating Margins (Lululemon Reportable Segments)

| Region | Jan 2022 | Jan 2023 | Jan 2024 | Feb 2025 | Trend |
|--------|----------|----------|----------|----------|-------|
| **Americas** | 35.23% | 38.49% | 38.04% | ~37% est. | Stable but under pressure |
| **China Mainland** | 38.53% | 34.15% | 37.00% | 37.45% | V-shaped recovery; now near Americas |
| **Rest of World** | 12.95% | ~18% | ~21% | 24.25% | Strong upward trajectory |

### Key Observations
1. **China margins are competitive with Americas** — 37.45% vs ~37% Americas, despite being a younger market with heavy store openings
2. **China margin dip in 2023** (34.15%) likely reflected COVID reopening investment + store ramp costs
3. **Recovery to 37.45%** suggests new stores reaching maturity quickly (positive unit economics signal)
4. **Rest of World catching up** from 13% to 24% — scaling benefits
5. **Americas margin pressure**: Revenue declined 1% in FY2025, comparable sales fell 3%, with Q4 acceleration of weakness (-4% revenue, -2% comps)

### Q4 FY2025 Margin Pressures (March 2026 Earnings)
- **Gross margin decreased 550 bps** due to tariff impacts and other factors
- Despite EPS beat ($5.01 vs $4.78 expected), margin compression is a concern
- **2026 guidance**: Americas revenue expected to decline 1-3%; China +20%; Rest of World mid-teens growth

### Analytical Implications
- China is approaching **margin parity with Americas** while growing 20-28% — this is the bull case for international shift
- If China reaches 20% of revenue at comparable margins, the Americas deceleration is partially offset
- **Tariff risk**: 550 bps gross margin hit shows vulnerability; China-sourced product faces US tariff exposure while China-sold product faces different cost dynamics
- **Four-wall margins**: Not explicitly disclosed by geography, but analyst concern exists that new China store openings with high occupancy/staffing costs could push young stores below four-wall thresholds

### Wilson's Key Arguments
- "The recent CEO change announcement was the third total failure of board oversight with no clear succession plan"

- Shareholders have no faith the board can select and support the next CEO without product experience input

### Lululemon's Defense

### Wilson's Position & Influence
- Founder; owns ~8-10% of shares (exact current holding unclear from search results)
- Cannot be ignored but also cannot unilaterally force changes
- **"Post-founder syndrome"** framing by Fortune (March 2026) — the tension between founder vision and institutional governance

### Intersection with Elliott
- **Two-front pressure**: Wilson (creative/brand) + Elliott (operational/CEO) are pushing from different angles
- **Potential alignment**: Both want leadership change, though their CEO preferences may differ
- **Potential conflict**: Wilson's nominees are creative/marketing-focused; Elliott's Nielsen is finance/operations-focused
- **Board math**: If Wilson gets 3 seats AND Elliott gets influence on CEO, the board dynamic shifts significantly

## Analytical Summary: Key Intersections

### Elliott at LULU — How B&N Playbook Maps

| B&N Element | LULU Analog | Confidence |
|-------------|------------|------------|
| Full acquisition | Minority activist stake (~$1B) | Different — less control |
| James Daunt as CEO | Jane Nielsen as CEO candidate | Direct parallel |
| Decentralized merchandising | Americas revitalization | Possible but different retail model |
| Store-as-community | Already LULU's DNA (sweatlife) | Not needed — already exists |
| Non-core divestiture (publishing) | Mirror brand sale? Product rationalization? | Speculative |
| Patient 7-year hold | Unknown timeline | Likely shorter for public company activism |

### China as LULU's Strategic Lever
- **Bull case**: China at 20% of revenue by 2026, growing 20%+, with Americas-comparable margins = the business is rebalancing itself regardless of CEO identity
- **Bear case**: Brand awareness still only mid-teens (vs 30s in US) means runway exists but execution risk is high; Anta/MAIA ACTIVE competitive pressure increasing; macro downtrade risk in Chinese consumer economy
- **Key metric to watch**: China four-wall margins as store count approaches 200; if new stores achieve comparable margins within 12-18 months, the unit economics thesis holds

### Two-Front Governance Pressure
- **Wilson** wants creative/product leadership (Marc Maurer from On Running)
- **Elliott** wants operational/financial leadership (Jane Nielsen from Ralph Lauren)
- **Resolution**: Could result in a "creative CEO + strong COO/CFO" structure, or one side prevailing
- **Timeline**: AGM ~June 2026 is the decision point; CEO appointment could come before or after
