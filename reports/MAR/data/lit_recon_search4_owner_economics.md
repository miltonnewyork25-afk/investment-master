## 1. Hotel GOP Margins by Tier (2024-2025)

| Chain Scale | GOP Margin (2025) | Trend | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Luxury** | ~36-38% | Flat/slight improvement | GOP +0.6pp without labor cost increase; ultra-luxury GOP% dipped 0.5pp as payroll rose 1.1pp |
| **Upscale** | 40.14% (Q3 2025) | Slight compression | Weaker pricing power in mid-chain; general benchmark 30-40% |
| **Upper Midscale** | **44.80%** (highest) | Declining | Peak 50.73% in May 2025; payroll +0.8pp, GOP% -1.3pp YoY |
| **Midscale/Economy** | ~41% (2024 champion) | Revenue-driven decline | GOP% and Payroll% both -0.2pp; margin loss from insufficient revenue, not overspending |
| **Select-Service/Extended-Stay** | >40% | Structurally advantaged | Simpler service models; greater room-revenue reliance |

**Key insight**: Select-service and upper midscale consistently outperform luxury on GOP% — critical for Marriott's asset-light model where fees are levied on owner revenues, but owner profitability drives willingness to invest/convert.

Industry average GOP%: 37.7% (H1 2025 actual) rising to 38.3% in Q4 2025. Budgeted vs actual variance was only 0.1pp, indicating strong forecasting discipline.

## 2. Hotel Labor Costs as % of Revenue (Post-COVID Trends)

| Year | Labor % of Revenue | Labor % of Total Expenses | Key Driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2019 (baseline) | ~29% | ~49% | Pre-COVID staffing levels |
| 2022 | 31.4% | 50.9% | Wage inflation + partial recovery |
| 2023 | 32.4% | 51.7% | +29.4% hourly comp vs 2019; -5.9% employees vs 2019 |
| 2024 | **34.4%** | — | Total compensation +4.8% YoY; F&B labor +15% (fastest) |
| 2025 | ~34-35% (est.) | — | Wage growth +5.9%; hours/occupied room -7% to -14.6% by dept |

**Structural shift quantified**:
- Hours worked per hotel: **-7.4%** vs 2019
- Compensation dollars paid: **+22.1%** vs 2019
- Average hospitality wage: $16.84 (2020) -> **$22.70** (early 2025), +34.8%
- Total accommodation sector compensation: **+26.5%** (2020-2024), outpacing CPI
- 70% of US hotels reduced/eliminated services (housekeeping, amenities) to cope

**2025 productivity gains** (Actabl data):
- Room attendants: 5.5% faster
- Guest service reps: 12.7% faster
- Management: ~14% faster
- Overall minutes/occupied room: -9%

**Staffing**: 67.6% of hoteliers reported shortages (Jan 2024 AHLA survey). Industry still ~200K workers below 2019 levels (2.17M vs 2.37M).

## 4. PIP (Property Improvement Plan) Costs Per Room

| Segment | PIP Cost/Room (Renovation) | New Build Cost/Room | PIP Cycle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Economy/Limited-Service | $10,000-$25,000 | $160,000-$170,000 | 7-15 years |
| Select-Service (Hampton, Courtyard) | **$20,000-$40,000+** | ~$250,000 | 5-7 years (Marriott) |
| Full-Service/Upscale | Varies widely; higher | ~$400,000+ | 5-7 years |
| Luxury | Significantly higher | **$1,000,000-$2,000,000+** | Case-by-case |

**Key data points**:
- PIP costs have increased **>30% vs pre-COVID levels** (2024)
- Hospitality vendor price hikes: **90-300%** on various products (steepest on economy items)
- Hampton "Forever Young" campaign: $20K-$30K/room (1990-95 builds); **>$40K/room** (1980s builds)
- IHG Holiday Inn Express Formula Blue 2.2: $10K-$25K/room ($940K-$2.6M total)
- RevPAR uplift post-PIP: avg **+9%**, up to **+17%** for higher-tier
- Rule of thumb: ~8% of revenues over 10-year period allocated to PIP
- Construction cost inflation: +4.69% (RLB, YE 2024); decelerating into 2025
- Brands increasingly open to negotiating PIP terms and timelines (2024-25)

|---|---|---|
| **Royalty/Franchise Fee** | **4-7% of gross room revenue** | Varies by brand tier |

## 6. Owner Satisfaction & Sentiment

### AHLA Survey (Sept 2025, ~400 respondents)
- **32%** delaying development projects
- **24%** scaling back projects
- **8%** canceling entirely
- Only **8%** moving forward with new investments
- **49%** report being understaffed
- **30%** seeing declines in completed leisure stays
- **26%** reporting drops in upcoming leisure bookings

### Franchise Fee Pressure
- Loyalty fees growing faster (+3.9%) than rooms occupied (+1.3%) or rooms revenue (+2.7%)
- Property-level costs for operations/maintenance, sales/marketing, and IT each rose ~5% in 2024
- Total franchise-related fees: 7-14% of gross revenue = significant drag on owner NOI

### J.D. Power (Guest Satisfaction, not Owner)
- J.D. Power does NOT publish a hotel owner/franchisee satisfaction survey
- Guest satisfaction 2025: avg 652/1000 (up from 647)
- Ritz-Carlton: 779 (luxury leader); Hampton: 694 (upper midscale)
- Marriott brands generally perform mid-pack in guest satisfaction rankings

### Franchise Business Review
- FBR is the primary source for franchisee satisfaction data (surveys 35,000+ franchisees across 375 brands)
- Hampton by Hilton: #1 lodging franchise for 17 consecutive years (Entrepreneur ranking)
- No publicly available Marriott-specific franchisee satisfaction scores found

### Owner Sentiment Indicators
- Rising fees + rising costs + flattening RevPAR = margin squeeze on owners
- AHLA successfully fought NLRB joint-employer rule (would have increased owner labor liability)
- Loyalty switching costs high: leaving Marriott = potentially losing 50%+ of bookings from 228M+ Bonvoy members

## 7. Conversion vs New-Build Economics

| Factor | Conversion | New Build |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost/Key** | PIP-level ($10K-$40K+/room) | $160K-$2M+/room by segment |
| **Time to Revenue** | Near-immediate (revenue stays "on") | 2-4 years construction |
| **ROI Profile** | Stronger (lower capital, faster payback) | Weaker in current rate environment |
| **Financing** | Easier (existing asset as collateral) | Lenders cautious; more equity required |
| **Supply Growth** | Accelerating | Decelerating |

**Quantitative context**:
- US hotel construction pipeline: 1,264 properties / 151,129 rooms (~2.6% of inventory, Nov 2024) — lowest since Aug 2022
- New supply growth: 0.2% (2023), 0.5% (2024), est. 0.8% (2025-26)
- IHG conversion signings nearly **doubled** between 2023-2024
- Spark by Hilton: 30+ locations, 175 in pipeline, many at **<$30K/room**
- Average hotel under construction: 118 rooms (2024) vs 132 rooms (2019) — projects getting smaller
- "The best deals from a typical ROI perspective are the conversion deals" — HVS

**Marriott conversion brands**: Tribute Portfolio, Autograph Collection, Delta Hotels — all positioned for conversion growth. Marriott added 123,000 rooms in 2024 (NUG 6.8%), pipeline ~577,000 rooms.

## 8. Key Money & Incentive Trends

**Marriott-specific**:
- Key money used on **~1/3 of all deals** (CEO Capuano)
- Deploying "slightly less key money per deal" but across **more tiers** including lower chain scales — "a bit of a new development"
- "The competitive landscape has really shifted toward key money being the tool of reference" — Capuano
- "We've got lots of financial tools in our toolbox for those deals that we think will provide outsized volumes of fees"

**Industry trends**:
- Major chains offering key money, credit enhancement, and other incentives especially for **mid-scale and extended-stay** brands
- Hilton incentives are contingent liabilities (not loans), offered at franchisor discretion
- Some brands offering financial incentives for completing specific PIP brand initiatives within deadlines
- Key money expanding beyond traditional luxury/upper-upscale into select-service and midscale

**Development pipeline context**:
- Marriott 2024: 123K rooms added, NUG 6.8%, pipeline 577K rooms
- Hilton 2024: ~100K rooms added (record), NUG 7.3%, pipeline 498.6K rooms
- Hilton lifestyle brands: 350 existing -> target 700 by 2028
- Franchise fees growing faster (+3.5%) than room revenue (+2.7%) — key money partially offsets to keep owners in system

Sources: [HOTELSMag Room Growth](https://hotelsmag.com/news/marriott-added-more-net-rooms-than-hilton-in-2024-does-it-matter/) | [White Sky Hospitality](https://whiteskyhospitality.com/the-hotel-owners-playbook-navigating-franchise-agreements-in-a-consolidating-market/) | [2025 Franchise Report](https://digital-issue.hoteliermagazine.com/articles/the-2025-franchise-report)

## Summary: Owner Economics Scorecard

| Dimension | Direction | Severity | MAR Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| GOP Margins | Compressing | Moderate | Owner profitability declining; limits fee increase capacity |
| Labor Costs | Rising structurally | High | +34.8% wages since 2020; partially offset by -7.4% hours |
| Insurance | Rising sharply | High | +19.5% YoY (2023); coastal/resort 2-5x average |
| PIP Costs | Rising >30% vs pre-COVID | Moderate-High | Pushes some owners out; creates conversion opportunity |
| Franchise Fees | Rising faster than revenue | Moderate | 7-14% total take rate; loyalty fees fastest-growing |
| Owner Sentiment | Deteriorating | Moderate | 64% delaying/scaling/canceling development |
| Conversion Economics | Favorable vs new-build | Positive for MAR | Accelerating conversion signings across industry |
| Key Money | Expanding to more tiers | Mixed | Cost to MAR balance sheet, but drives pipeline growth |

**Net assessment**: Hotel owners face a margin squeeze from all directions (labor, insurance, PIP costs, franchise fees) while revenue growth flattens. This creates both risk (owner pushback on fees, slower development) and opportunity (conversion economics favor asset-light franchise model; loyalty switching costs lock owners in). Marriott's ~7-14% total fee take is sustainable as long as Bonvoy delivers superior RevPAR to owners, but the gap between fee growth (+3.5%) and revenue growth (+2.7%) cannot persist indefinitely without owner friction.
