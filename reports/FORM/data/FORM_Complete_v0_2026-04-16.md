## 0. Executive Thesis

FormFactor is probably not being misunderstood because investors think it has no AI relevance. It is more likely being misunderstood because the market is already valuing it against a cleaner, broader, post-expansion operating model than the company actually has today.

The current evidence supports four linked judgments:

1. `FORM` has real advanced-memory / HBM / high-performance-compute test exposure.
2. That exposure currently looks narrower and more concentrated than a broad "AI infrastructure beneficiary" label suggests.
3. The company's margin and free-cash-flow transition is plausible, but still heavily conditioned on execution, capex absorption, and demand duration.
4. The stock already appears priced as if much of that transition risk is behind it rather than ahead of it.

Working report center of gravity:

> FORM may be a good business entering a potentially better operating phase, but the stock seems to be pricing tomorrow's operating model more aggressively than today's economic evidence warrants.

## 3. Report Spine

FormFactor is best understood as a capital-intensive test and measurement company spanning probe cards, engineering systems, and advanced test complexity across the semiconductor lifecycle.

The market may be valuing FORM as if:

- HBM-linked test intensity is broad
- the margin/FCF transition is near

## 6. Competition And Moat

### 6.1 The moat exists, but it is narrower than the stock may imply

The official `10-K` supports a nuanced moat view.

What looks real:

- meaningful barriers to entry in advanced `MEMS` probe cards
- long and expensive customer qualification cycles
- customer-specific customization
- signal-integrity and high-density performance in difficult applications
- strong customer-support and roadmap-integration capability

What tempers the story:

- the probe-card market is still fragmented
- some competitors use similar `MEMS` approaches
- the company is not dependent on a single irreplaceable patent
- customers can alter test strategies in ways that reduce test content or shift requirements

Best current framing:

> FORM appears to have a medium-strength niche moat, especially in advanced probe cards and high-complexity test applications, but not a monopoly-like moat that should automatically justify paying any premium at any time.

### 6.2 Substitution risk matters more than many investors may realize

The company explicitly warns that customers may:

- reduce the need for high-end probe content
- adopt lower-performance testers
- change methodology in ways that reduce the use of FORM products

That means the moat is not just about current product strength.
It is also about whether customers continue to need this intensity of test.

## 7. Scenario Valuation Pressure Test

### 7.1 The key question

The most useful valuation question is not "what is a fair multiple today?"

It is:

- how much operating success is already embedded in the current stock price?

### 7.2 What the pressure test says

At roughly a `$10B` current equity value:

- `$70M` FCF implies only about `0.7%` FCF yield
- `$110M` FCF implies only about `1.1%` FCF yield
- `$160M` FCF implies only about `1.6%` FCF yield
- `$190M` FCF implies only about `1.9%` FCF yield

This is a very demanding setup.

It implies that even full target-model execution may only justify today's price if investors continue to assign FORM a premium quality multiple after the transition.

### 7.3 Practical interpretation

This is why the stock feels fragile from a valuation perspective:

- it does not need a business collapse to de-rate
- it only needs the conversion to be slower, narrower, or less complete than the current price assumes

Working judgment:

> FORM may be good enough to deserve strategic attention, but the stock appears priced much closer to "full conversion" than to "promising transition."

## 9. Segment Economics

### 9.1 FORM is still overwhelmingly a probe-card company

The official segment data is decisive here.

In FY2025:

- `Probe Cards` revenue was about `$637.9M`
- `Systems` revenue was about `$147.1M`

That means:

- `Probe Cards` represented about `81%` of total revenue
- `Systems` represented only about `19%`

This is crucial for valuation language.

It means that even if the company has multiple strategic narratives, the business is still economically dominated by one segment.

### 9.2 The mix shift toward DRAM matters

Within probe cards, the official filing shows:

- `Foundry & Logic` share fell from `60.9%` of Probe Card sales in FY2024 to `58.0%` in FY2025
- `DRAM` rose from `36.3%` to `38.8%`

Management also says:

- `DRAM` products generally carry lower margins than `Foundry & Logic`

This is one of the most important underappreciated tensions in the story.

Why:

- rising DRAM / HBM relevance helps the demand narrative
- but that same mix shift does not automatically maximize margins

So the market may be rewarding the company for the same thing that complicates the margin story.

### 9.3 Systems is strategically useful, but not yet a premium re-rating engine

The systems segment broadens the story into:

- probe stations
- thermal systems
- cryogenic systems

That matters strategically.

But the economics are still mixed:

- systems gross margin fell from `51.3%` in FY2023 to `41.8%` in FY2025
- systems remains below one-fifth of revenue

Working interpretation:

> Systems helps FORM's strategic optionality, but it does not yet justify treating the company like a broad premium platform on its own.

## 10. CQ Answers

### CQ1. What is FORM really being paid for by the market today?

The market is mainly paying for future margin and FCF quality rather than trailing economics.

### CQ2. Is the current growth wave broad, or mostly HBM / DRAM concentrated?

The current evidence points to a real but narrower, more memory-heavy surge rather than a fully broad-based wave.

### CQ3. Can FORM convert demand strength into durable margins and free cash flow?

Plausibly yes, but not yet conclusively. The bridge is real, but still long and execution-heavy.

### CQ4. Is the balance sheet a strength, or does capital intensity still constrain the story?

The balance sheet is a strength. Capital intensity is still the main economic constraint.

### CQ5. Does signaling weaken the clean bull case?

Yes, modestly. The insider-net-selling signal and target-model timing gap complicate the cleanest bull framing.

### CQ6. What peer set best explains the multiple?

No single peer set is perfect. That mismatch itself is part of the thesis: the market is borrowing language from stronger quality peers than FORM has yet earned economically.

### CQ7. What could break the thesis over the next 4-6 quarters?

Revenue breadth, gross-margin durability, concentration trends, and `Farmers Branch` execution are the main decision points.

### Integrated CQ Judgment

> FORM is a real and strategically relevant advanced-test company, but the stock already assumes that narrow demand strength can be converted into broad, durable, premium-quality economics. That may happen, but the current valuation leaves limited room for a slower or messier path.
