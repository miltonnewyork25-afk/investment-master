## 3. What This Table Says

### 3.1 FORM is too expensive for a plain "small cyclical equipment" reading

Compared with `KLIC` and `AEHR`:

- FORM trades at a clear premium on `EV/Sales`
- FORM's `FCF Yield` is dramatically lower than `KLIC`
- FORM's current economics are better than `AEHR`, but not so superior that the premium is trivial

Interpretation:

- the market is not valuing FORM as a plain lower-tier cyclical hardware name

### 3.2 FORM does not yet look like a proven large-platform semi-cap quality leader

Compared with `LRCX`, `AMAT`, and `KLAC`:

- FORM's `EV/Sales` is already in the same broad neighborhood
- but FORM's `ROIC` is vastly lower
- FORM's `FCF Yield` is far weaker
- FORM's `CapEx / Revenue` is materially heavier than top-tier platform peers

Interpretation:

- FORM is carrying a valuation language that begins to rhyme with large semi-cap quality names
- but its current economic output is nowhere near those quality levels

### 3.3 TER is the uncomfortable comparison

`TER` is useful because it shows what a higher-quality test-exposure company can look like:

- higher `EV/Sales`
- similar-ish `EV/EBITDA`
- much better `ROIC`
- stronger `FCF` profile

Interpretation:

- if the market wants to price FORM as a high-quality test-intensity compounder, it still needs much more proof

## 4. Provisional Valuation Language

The wrong valuation language:

- "generic AI infrastructure beneficiary"
- "mini LRCX"
- "simple picks-and-shovels winner"

The more accurate valuation language:

> FORM is a capital-intensive test-exposure company with real HBM / advanced-memory torque, but it is currently being valued closer to where a much more proven quality transition would deserve to trade.

Even sharper:

> FORM is not obviously mispriced because the business is weak. It may be mispriced because the market is paying today's multiple for tomorrow's operating model.

---

## 5. Implication For The Main Report

This peer work strengthens the central contradiction:

- the market is clearly giving FORM credit for scarcity and future quality
- but the current return, cash-flow, and capital-intensity profile still looks transitional

That means the main report should not ask:

- "Is FORM a good company?"

It should ask:

- "Has the market already priced FORM as if the hard operational conversion has already happened?"
