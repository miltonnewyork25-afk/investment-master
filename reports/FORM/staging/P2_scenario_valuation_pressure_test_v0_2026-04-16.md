# FORM P2 Scenario Valuation Pressure Test v0

> Date: 2026-04-16
> Purpose: test how much operating success is already embedded in FORM's current market value

Primary source base:

- official target model
- official FY2025 results
- repo live quote and statement data
- peer framework already built in earlier FORM modules

---

## 1. Why A Pressure Test Matters More Than A Traditional DCF Here

At this stage, a classic long-horizon DCF would create false precision.

The more useful question is simpler:

> What level of free cash flow and quality multiple does FORM need in order for today's stock price to make sense?

That is the real valuation problem.

---

## 2. Current Context

Using the current live quote stack, FORM's equity value is roughly:

- about `$10B`
- roughly `$128` per share equivalent

Using official company numbers:

- FY2025 revenue: about `$785M`
- management target-model revenue: about `$850M`
- FY2025 free cash flow: about `$14M`
- simple ex-`Farmers Branch` normalization: about `$69M`
- target-model free cash flow: about `$160M`

---

## 3. The Most Important Observation

At roughly a `$10B` current equity value:

- if FORM only earns `$70M` of FCF, the implied FCF yield is only about `0.7%`
- if FORM earns `$110M` of FCF, the implied FCF yield is only about `1.1%`
- if FORM fully hits the `$160M` target model, the implied FCF yield is still only about `1.6%`
- even at `$190M` FCF, the implied FCF yield is only about `1.9%`

That is the heart of the issue.

Even success may already be heavily pre-paid.

---

## 4. Revenue-Multiple Pressure

If current enterprise value is roughly `$9.9B`:

- on FY2025 revenue (`785M`), EV / Sales is about `12.6x`
- on target-model revenue (`850M`), EV / Sales is still about `11.7x`
- even on a more optimistic `900M` revenue level, EV / Sales is still about `11.0x`

Interpretation:

- the current valuation is not just rich on current numbers
- it remains rich even on the official target-model revenue base

That means the market is not paying for scale alone.
It is paying for premium quality.

---

## 5. Scenario Table

This is not a price target table.
It is a pressure test of what today's value requires.

### Bear / Delay Case

Assume:

- FCF settles around `$70M`
- justified FCF yield ranges from `2.5%` to `3.5%`

Implied value:

- about `$26-$36` per share

### Base / Partial Conversion Case

Assume:

- FCF reaches about `$110M`
- justified FCF yield ranges from `1.75%` to `2.5%`

Implied value:

- about `$56-$81` per share

### Full Target Model Case

Assume:

- FCF reaches about `$160M`
- justified FCF yield ranges from `1.5%` to `2.0%`

Implied value:

- about `$103-$137` per share

### Above-Target Bull Case

Assume:

- FCF reaches about `$190M`
- justified FCF yield ranges from `1.35%` to `1.75%`

Implied value:

- about `$139-$181` per share

---

## 6. What This Table Really Says

The current stock price sits uncomfortably high in the conversion stack.

In plain terms:

- if FORM only partially converts, today's price is hard to defend
- if FORM fully reaches the target model, today's price can be defended only if investors are still willing to accept a very premium FCF yield
- if FORM materially exceeds the target model, then upside can exist from here

This means:

> the current stock is not priced for "good progress." It is priced much closer to "full conversion plus premium quality multiple."

---

## 7. Why This Is So Important For The Main Report

This pressure test strengthens the central contradiction more than any ordinary valuation ratio could.

It suggests:

- FORM may not need to fail for the stock to disappoint
- it may merely need to convert more slowly, or less completely, than the current valuation assumes

That is a very different risk profile from a stock that is simply misunderstood or ignored.

---

## 8. Working Conclusion

The right valuation conclusion is not:

- "FORM is obviously absurdly priced"

Nor is it:

- "the market is clearly right"

It is:

> FORM's current price appears to assume that the company's target-model bridge largely works, that the business earns a premium quality multiple afterward, and that the market never materially de-risks the valuation during the transition. That is possible, but it is a demanding setup rather than an easy one.
