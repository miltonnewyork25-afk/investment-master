# FORM P1 Farmers Branch Margin / FCF Bridge

> Date: 2026-04-16
> Purpose: test whether management's target model is being interpreted too optimistically relative to the actual expansion and cash-flow path

Primary source base:

- FY2025 `10-K`
- Q4 2025 investor presentation
- repo live `FMP` data

---

## 1. The Core Setup

Management's target model presents a very attractive end state:

- revenue: `785M -> 850M`
- non-GAAP gross margin: `40.8% -> 47.0%`
- non-GAAP operating margin: `13.4% -> 22.0%`
- free cash flow: `14M -> 160M`

That sounds like a modest-growth but major-quality-transition story.

The key issue is timing:

- the company is simultaneously building and ramping `Farmers Branch`
- fiscal `2026` start-up costs are expected at `20M-25M`
- fiscal `2026` capex is expected at `140M-170M`

So the path to that end state is not smooth.

---

## 2. What The Historical Bridge Actually Looks Like

Using the repo's live financial data:

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2020 | 693.6 | 41.5% | 12.1% | 16.3% | 8.1% |
| 2021 | 769.7 | 41.9% | 12.7% | 9.5% | 8.6% |
| 2022 | 747.9 | 39.6% | 7.3% | 8.9% | 8.7% |
| 2023 | 663.1 | 39.0% | 12.5% | 1.3% | 8.4% |
| 2024 | 763.6 | 40.3% | 8.5% | 10.4% | 5.0% |
| 2025 | 785.0 | 39.5% | 8.5% | 1.5% | 13.2% |

This table matters a lot.

It shows:

- gross margin has not yet broken out
- operating margin in `2025` was still only `8.5%`
- free cash flow collapsed back to a very low level in `2025`
- capex intensity spiked sharply in `2025`

Interpretation:

- the target model is not describing what the business currently looks like
- it is describing what management believes the business can become after a difficult transition

---

## 3. Why Farmers Branch Changes The Reading

The single most important distortion in the current numbers is `Farmers Branch`.

Known facts:

- FY2025 total capex was about `$103.7M`
- about `$55.0M` of that was tied to `Farmers Branch`
- official FY2025 free cash flow in company materials was about `$14M`

Simple normalization:

- reported FCF: about `$14M`
- add back `Farmers Branch` capex: about `+$55M`
- normalized FCF ex-Farmers Branch: about `$69M`

That normalized figure is much better than the headline reported number.

But it still only implies:

- normalized FCF margin of about `8.8%`

That is still far below:

- target-model FCF of `$160M`
- target-model FCF margin of about `20.4%` on the current revenue base

Interpretation:

- the bull case is not fake
- but the gap between today's economics and the target model remains very large even after adjusting for the most obvious distortion

---

## 4. The Most Important Framing Error To Avoid

The target model can easily be misread as if it were a near-term destination.

But there is a major comparability issue:

- the target-model capex of `30M-35M` is presented excluding `Farmers Branch`
- the actual transition period still includes large real cash outflows for that expansion

This means:

- the target model is closer to a post-build steady-state picture
- it is not the same thing as the next one or two years of reported economics

That distinction is critical for valuation.

If investors are using the target model as if it were just around the corner, they may be paying too much too early.

---

## 5. What This Means For The Main Thesis

The central bridge question becomes:

> Can FORM hold demand strong enough, long enough, to move from a roughly `9%` normalized FCF margin toward a `20%+` target-model FCF margin while still absorbing expansion costs and execution risk?

That is a much harder question than:

- "Does FORM benefit from AI and HBM?"

The answer to the easy question is probably yes.

The answer to the hard question is not proven yet.

---

## 6. Working Conclusion

`Farmers Branch` is not a side detail. It is one of the main reasons the valuation debate is difficult.

It does two things at once:

- it gives bulls a plausible explanation for why current FCF understates future earnings power
- it gives bears a plausible explanation for why the transition from story to economics may take longer and cost more than the market wants to assume

Working judgment:

> FORM is being valued against a cleaner, steadier, post-expansion operating model than the company actually has today. The next phase of research should test whether the demand duration is strong enough to justify that optimism.
