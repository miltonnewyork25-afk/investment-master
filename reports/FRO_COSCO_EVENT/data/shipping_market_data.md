## 2. Forward Curve / Futures Market

- **Crude oil curve**: Shifted from backwardation to **demand-driven contango** since Q4 2025 (futures priced above spot)
- **2026-2028 outlook**: Expanding global crude balance expected to weigh on front-end pricing; resilient EM demand supports back-end
- **McQuilling forecast** (pre-crisis): VLCC ECO non-scrubber spot earnings averaging **$87,000/day** over 2026-2027
- **Contango implication**: Higher storage economics could incentivize floating storage, temporarily removing VLCC tonnage from spot market → rate-supportive
- **Current disconnect**: War-driven spot rates ($100K-$600K) vastly exceed forward expectations ($87K), implying market prices a geopolitical premium that may not persist

- ~100 LPG carriers also in Iranian trades (expanding beyond oil)
- **OFAC actions (Feb 2026)**: 12 tankers sanctioned + 30+ individuals/entities designated + 14 additional vessels identified as blocked property (State Dept)

### Market Impact

## 6. Strait of Hormuz Traffic Data

### Timeline
| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| Feb 28, 2026 | US/Israeli military operations begin |
| Mar 2 | **IRGC confirms strait closure**; threatens all ships |
| Mar 5 | Iran announces selective closure: blocked to US/Israel/Western allies only |
| Mar 8 | Selective passage policy reconfirmed |
| Mar 1-15 | **11 China-linked vessels** transit (mostly general cargo) |
| **Mar 15** | **First full day with ZERO commercial traffic** in either direction |
| Mar 16 | US Treasury Sec. Bessent says US allowing Iranian tankers through |
| Mar 18 | Traffic described as "trickling" — 21 tankers total since Feb 28 |

### Key Numbers
- **Pre-conflict**: 100+ ships/day transiting
- **Since Feb 28**: Only **21 tankers** total in ~18 days (vs ~1,800+ normal)
- **~400 ships** waiting in Gulf of Oman (holding position, not dispersing — expecting reopening)
- **20% of global oil** normally transits Hormuz
- Iran issuing selective passage: China/neutral flags allowed; Western flags blocked

### Implications for Tanker Market
1. **Effective tonnage shortage**: ~400 waiting ships = stranded capacity → spot market squeeze
2. **Rerouting premium**: Ships avoiding Hormuz must take Cape of Good Hope route (+10-15 days)
3. **Two-tier market forming**: China-linked/neutral tonnage can access Gulf; Western-flagged cannot
4. **FRO impact**: Frontline's fleet predominantly Western-linked → higher war risk costs, potential inability to access MEG loading
5. **COSCO/中远海能 advantage**: Chinese-flagged vessels may have preferential Hormuz access

## Summary: Market Regime Assessment

| Factor | Status | Direction for Rates |
|--------|--------|-------------------|
| Spot rates | $112K-$601K (extreme vol) | Bullish (crisis premium) |
| War risk insurance | 20-50x increase | Bullish (cost pass-through) |
| Hormuz traffic | ~98% reduction | Bullish (tonnage stranded) |
| Forward curve | Contango (pre-crisis $87K avg) | Bearish (market expects normalization) |
| Orderbook | 15% of fleet; 60-70/yr in 2027-28 | Bearish (medium-term supply wave) |
| Shadow fleet sanctions | ~1,750+ ships; OFAC accelerating | Bullish (removes tonnage from compliant market) |
| Second-hand prices | Decade highs | Confirms tight market expectations |

**Net assessment**: The VLCC market is in an unprecedented crisis-driven super-spike. Near-term rates are dominated by the Hormuz closure (bullish). Medium-term (2027-28), the orderbook wave + potential conflict resolution could normalize rates toward the $60-90K range. The key variable is **duration of the Hormuz disruption** — every additional week of closure compounds the tonnage shortage and extends the rate premium.
