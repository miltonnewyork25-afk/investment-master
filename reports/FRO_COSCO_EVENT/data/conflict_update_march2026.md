## 4. P&I Insurance & War Risk Premiums

### P&I Club Cancellations (Effective ~March 5, 2026)

Members of the **International Group of P&I Clubs** ceased war-risk insurance for vessels operating in/around Iran, including the Strait of Hormuz and Persian Gulf. Confirmed cancellations from:
- **Gard**
- **Skuld**
- **NorthStandard**
- **London P&I Club**
- **American Club**
- **Steamship Mutual**

All cancellations took effect with short notice, generally around **March 5, 2026**.

### War Risk Premium Escalation

| Period | Rate (% of hull value) | Per Transit Cost Impact |
|--------|----------------------|------------------------|
| Pre-conflict | **0.125%** | Baseline |
| Early March | **0.2%-0.4%** | +$250K per VLCC transit |
| Current (mid-March) | **1%-5%** (up to 10% for specific flags) | Prohibitive for most |
| Renewal period | **Every 7 days** | Continuous repricing risk |

### Marine Hull Insurance

Marsh estimates marine hull insurance rates in the Gulf could rise **25-50%** due to the Iran conflict.

### Insurance Gap — The Core Problem

- **329 vessels** currently operating in Persian Gulf (JPMorgan estimate)
- Each requires oil pollution, salvage, hull, and third-party liability coverage
- Implied total coverage need: **~$352 billion** — far beyond private market capacity
- Without liability coverage, even hull-insured ships won't sail (crew, pollution, third-party risks uninsured)

## 5. Chubb / DFC $20B Maritime Reinsurance Plan

### Program Structure

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Announced** | 2026-03-07 (plan) / 2026-03-11 (Chubb as lead) |
| **Lead Agency** | US International Development Finance Corporation (DFC) |
| **Lead Underwriter** | **Chubb** (global P&C insurer, Political Risk + Maritime expertise) |
| **Coverage Cap** | **$20 billion** on a rolling basis |
| **Scope** | Hull & Machinery + Cargo (initial focus) |
| **Reinsurers** | Several American insurance companies behind Chubb alongside DFC |
| **Goal** | Resume commercial shipping through Strait of Hormuz |

### Critical Limitation: Liability Gap

Per **Moody's Ratings** and **Insurance Journal** (2026-03-16):
- The plan covers **hull & machinery + cargo only**
- **No liability coverage** (oil pollution, crew injury, third-party damage)
- Without liability cover, **shipowners still won't send vessels** through the Strait
- JPMorgan estimates **$352 billion** total coverage needed vs. $20B provided = **5.7% of requirement**
- **Moody's Ratings** (2026-03-16): Explicitly stated "without coverage of liability risk, the plan isn't likely to work"
- **Verdict**: Plan is necessary but **insufficient** to restart Gulf shipping in its current form

### DFC Background

The DFC is a development agency typically focused on emerging-market infrastructure, not wartime maritime insurance. Its deployment in this role is unprecedented and reflects the severity of the insurance market failure.

## 6. OFAC Iran Shipping Sanctions

### Recent Enforcement Actions (2025-2026)

- **2025 total**: OFAC sanctioned **875+ persons, vessels, and aircraft** targeting Iran's oil export network
- **By Dec 2025**: **180+ vessels** sanctioned for shipping Iranian petroleum/products
- **Feb 2026**: Treasury targeted **12 shadow fleet vessels** + owners/operators (hundreds of millions in Iranian petroleum transported)
- **Feb 27, 2026**: Federal Register published additional OFAC sanctions actions
- **March 2026**: Treasury sanctioned **29 additional shadow fleet vessels** + management firms

### Focus Areas

1. **Shadow fleet** — Vessels transporting Iranian petroleum to foreign markets (primary revenue source for regime)
2. **IRGC supply networks** — Entities enabling procurement of precursor materials for ballistic missiles
3. **MODAFL weapons networks** — Advanced conventional weapons production supply chains
4. **Dark fleet overlap** — Crude carriers previously engaged in Venezuela sanctions evasion now targeted

### Impact Assessment

- Sanctions are **driving up costs** for Iranian oil exporters and **reducing per-barrel revenue**
- However, shadow fleet continues to operate, suggesting enforcement gaps remain
- War context may shift focus from sanctions to kinetic disruption of Iranian exports

## 8. OPEC+ Response

### Production Decision (March 1, 2026)

- OPEC+ agreed to increase output by **206,000 bpd** starting April 2026
- Higher than the **137,000 bpd** expert forecast
- Official statement cited "steady global economic outlook and healthy market fundamentals" (no explicit mention of Iran war)

### Saudi Arabia Pre-Positioning

- Saudi Arabia **drove OPEC output higher ahead of conflict** (Bloomberg survey, 2026-03-13)
- Tanker tracking data showed Riyadh and other producers **ramped up exports in weeks leading up to** the Feb 28 strikes
- This suggests Saudi Arabia had advance knowledge or expectation of the conflict

### Pre-Conflict Production Surge (New in v2)

- OPEC crude output rose **640,000 bpd** in February as Saudi Arabia increased production **ahead of** the conflict escalation (Bloomberg survey, 2026-03-13)
- This strongly suggests Saudi Arabia had advance knowledge or expectation of the US-Israeli strikes

### IEA Strategic Reserve Release (New in v2)

- IEA member nations (US, Japan, Germany, etc.) agreed to release **400 million barrels** from emergency strategic petroleum reserves — described as **unprecedented** in scale
- For context: the 2022 Russia-Ukraine SPR release was 182M bbl; this is 2.2x larger

### Assessment: Response is Inadequate

| Factor | Magnitude |
|--------|-----------|
| OPEC+ increase | **206K bpd** |
| Iran pre-war production | **~3.2M bpd** |
| Hormuz transit volume | **~17-21M bpd** (20% of global supply) |
| Effective disruption | **Millions of bpd** (exact unknown, depends on selective passage) |
| **Gap** | **OPEC+ increase covers ~1% of disrupted volume** |

- Rystad Energy analyst warned the increase was "potentially not large enough to prevent an oil price spike"
- Saudi Arabia's Red Sea port **Yanbu** is seeing increased VLCC loadings as a Gulf alternative, but capacity is limited

## Summary: Key Takeaways for FRO/COSCO Event-Driven Analysis

### Bullish Tanker Factors (FRO/Frontline, COSCO Shipping Energy)

1. **VLCC rates at all-time highs**: $424K-$770K/day vs. ~$40-60K/day normalized
2. **Strait closure duration uncertain**: No ceasefire path visible; both sides rejecting talks
3. **Insurance market failure**: Even if Strait partially reopens, insurance costs add $250K+ per transit

5. **DFC plan insufficient**: $20B covers hull only, no liability — ships still won't sail without full coverage
6. **OPEC+ response token**: 206K bpd vs. millions disrupted

### Bearish / Risk Factors

1. **Ceasefire could come suddenly**: Back-channel contacts exist (Iran FM texting US envoy)
2. **Selective passage**: Iran allowing some non-Western ships through — partial reopening possible
3. **US naval coalition**: If successful, could provide military escort for commercial shipping
4. **Rate collapse risk**: Current rates are war-premium driven; normalization would be swift and brutal
5. **Demand destruction**: $100+ oil will slow global economic growth, reducing oil/shipping demand over time
6. **Sanctions overhang**: OFAC shadow fleet targeting could complicate COSCO operations if any linked entities are sanctioned

### Key Metrics to Monitor

- Windward daily Hormuz transit counts (currently near zero for inbound)
- Baltic TD3C index (currently $280K-$424K/day)
