## 2. Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) Analysis

### Q1 FY2026 SBC: $2.176B (11.3% of revenue)

Breakdown by function:
| Category | Q1 FY2026 | Q1 FY2025 |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| Cost of Revenue | $236M | — |
| R&D | $1,447M | — |
| SG&A | $493M | — |
| **Total SBC** | **$2,176M** | **$1,280M** |

**Key observations**:
- SBC grew 70% YoY ($1.28B → $2.18B), significantly faster than revenue growth (29%)
- SBC as % of revenue: ~11.3% in Q1 FY2026 vs ~8.5% in Q1 FY2025 — trending UP, not normalizing
- R&D accounts for 66% of total SBC, reflecting heavy investment in AI/custom silicon talent
- GAAP-to-Non-GAAP gap: $2.84B in Q1 FY2026 (operating income), of which $2.18B is SBC
- Unrecognized SBC as of May 2025: $27.0B (multi-year overhang)
- Post-VMware retention grants (2-year time + market-based RSUs, staggered 2025-2026 vesting) contribute to elevated SBC

**Normalization trajectory**: Despite some analyst expectations of SBC declining to ~10% post-VMware integration, Q1 FY2026 data shows the opposite — SBC is rising both in absolute terms and as a percentage of revenue. The $27B unrecognized balance suggests elevated SBC will persist through at least FY2027.

**Analytical implication for Tier 3**: SBC-adjusted FCF is critical. The ~$2.2B/quarter gap between GAAP and non-GAAP significantly affects true owner earnings calculation.

## 3. Valuation Debate: Bull vs Bear at $1.5T+

**Current**: ~$1.505T market cap, ~$330/share, ~65x trailing earnings

### Bull Case
- **AI TAM explosion**: Hock Tan projects AI chip revenue "significantly in excess of $100B" by 2027; Broadcom is the dominant custom ASIC partner for Google (TPU), Meta, and others
- **Hybrid asset**: High-margin software (VMware) + explosive AI growth = unique combination
- **Analyst consensus**: 29 Buys / 2 Holds, average PT $458 (implies +38% upside)
- **FCF machine**: 41% FCF margin, $8B/quarter; at annualized ~$32B FCF, trades at ~47x FCF
- **VMware synergies**: Software segment delivering 68%+ EBITDA margins post-integration
- **Custom silicon moat**: 90% market share in custom AI ASICs, deep multi-year design wins

### Bear Case
- **Valuation stretch**: ~65x earnings, 26% overvalued vs intrinsic value in base scenarios (Alpha Spread)
- **Customer concentration**: Revenue heavily dependent on 3-4 hyperscalers (Google, Meta, Microsoft, ByteDance); any pullback or internalization = material revenue hit
- **Internalization risk**: DA Davidson flags hyperscalers may bring more silicon design in-house over time, pressuring supplier economics
- **AI ASIC competition**: Marvell as direct competitor; AWS (Trainium) and Microsoft developing internal chips = "make vs buy" threat
- **Software deceleration**: VMware/infrastructure software grew only +1% YoY in Q1 — organic growth may be exhausted post-migration bump
- **Cyclical exposure**: If hyperscaler AI capex slows in 2026-2027 (capacity absorption phase), Broadcom's semiconductor revenue is vulnerable
- **SBC dilution**: 11.3% SBC/revenue and rising — real earnings growth is lower than non-GAAP suggests
- **Geopolitical**: Export control risks, supply chain exposure to TSMC

## 5. Hock Tan Succession Planning & Management Depth

**CEO profile**: Hock E. Tan, age 73 (born 1953), President & CEO since Avago era, widely regarded as one of the most efficient capital allocators in corporate history. Known for "ruthless efficiency" — slashing overhead at acquired companies to drive margins to 60%+.

**Contract**: Board extended Tan's contract through at least 2030 in late 2025, providing 4+ years of CEO continuity.

**Succession risk factors**:
- **Key-man dependency**: Broadcom's strategy (serial acquisition + cost optimization + strategic pivots to AI) is deeply tied to Tan's personal judgment and relationships
- **Board engagement**: FY2024 say-on-pay received only 61% approval; board responded with enhanced disclosure on CEO succession planning
- **Management bench**: Limited public visibility on internal successor candidates; CFO Kirsten Spears and key division heads are known but not prominently positioned as successors
- **Operational risk**: Tan's unique skill — identifying and executing transformative M&A (LSI, Broadcom, Brocade, CA Technologies, Symantec, VMware) — is difficult to replicate
- **Mitigant**: The 2030 contract extension buys time, and VMware integration is largely complete, reducing near-term execution dependency

**Analytical implication**: For a Tier 3 analysis, Hock Tan succession should be modeled as a discrete risk event. A 10-15% "key-man discount" may be warranted in bear scenarios, particularly post-2030.

## Summary: Key Tensions for Deep Analysis

1. **SBC trajectory is the critical quality-of-earnings question**: 11.3% and rising, not normalizing. The $27B unrecognized overhang means years of elevated dilution. Non-GAAP earnings overstate true owner economics by ~25%.

2. **AI growth vs software stall**: Semiconductor segment (+52%) is masking infrastructure software deceleration (+1%). VMware's organic growth story may be exhausted.

3. **Customer concentration + internalization**: Bull case assumes durable ASIC partnerships; bear case assumes hyperscalers will eventually in-house. Truth is likely in between — but the timing matters enormously at 65x earnings.

4. **Valuation requires perfection**: At ~1.5% SBC-adjusted FCF yield, there is minimal room for execution miss. The bull case requires AI revenue to reach $40B+ annualized by FY2027 AND SBC to stabilize.

5. **Hock Tan = Berkshire-like key-man risk**: Contract through 2030, but no visible successor. This is a long-duration risk that grows with time.
