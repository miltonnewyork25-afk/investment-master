## 1. Business Segments — Full Year 2025 Performance

Eaton operates 5 reportable segments. FY2025 total revenue: **$27.4B** (record), up 10% YoY.
Organic growth contributed 8pp, acquisitions contributed 2pp. Record segment margins of **24.5%**.

Post-spin (announced Jan 2026), the company will effectively have 3 core segments (Electrical Americas, Electrical Global, Aerospace) generating ~$24B at ~26% margins.

### Segment Dynamics

**Fastest Growing:**
1. **Electrical Americas** — Dominant growth engine. 15% organic growth in Q4 2025, accelerating from 7% organic in Q3. Data center orders surging +200% YoY. Backlog of $13.2B provides multi-year revenue visibility. Management targets 30% margins by end of 2026, 32% by 2030. At current build rates, the U.S. large data center construction backlog spans ~11 years.
2. **Aerospace** — 12-14% organic growth, driven by commercial aviation recovery and defense spending. Backlog $4.3B, orders +11% on rolling 12-month basis. 24.1% margins with 120bps YoY expansion.
3. **Electrical Global** — Solid 6% organic growth with 200bps margin expansion to 19.7%. Backlog +19% YoY.

**Declining:**
1. **Vehicle** — Revenue declining 9-13% organically, margin contracting 230bps. Secular headwinds from ICE powertrain transition.
2. **eMobility** — Revenue declining 15%, though margins improving (600bps expansion to 7.8%). EV adoption slower than expected, but profitability inflecting.

## 2. M&A History — Major Acquisitions

### Landmark Deals

| Year | Target | Price | Strategic Rationale | Revenue Contribution |
|------|--------|-------|--------------------|--------------------|
| **2012** | Cooper Industries | **$11.8B** (equity value, ~$13B EV) | Transformative — created global power management leader. Added electrical distribution, lighting, safety products | Doubled Eaton's electrical business |
| **2021** | Tripp Lite | **$1.65B** | UPS/power protection for edge computing, data centers | ~$500M revenue at acquisition |
| **2022** | Royal Power Solutions | **$600M** (~13.6x EBITDA) | Electrical connectors for eMobility, renewables | Bolt-on to Electrical segment |
| **2025** | Fibrebond | **$1.4B** | Pre-integrated modular power enclosures for data centers | Est. $378M revenue, $110M adj. EBITDA |
| **2025** | Resilient Power Systems | Undisclosed | EV charging infrastructure solutions | Bolt-on |
| **2025** | Ultra PCS | **$1.55B** | Power conversion systems for defense/aerospace | Bolt-on to Aerospace |
| **2025 (announced)** | Boyd Thermal | **$9.5B** (~22.5x est. 2026 EBITDA) | Liquid cooling technology for data centers | Pending close — largest deal since Cooper |

### 2025 M&A Summary
Management described **$13 billion in strategic capital allocation** in 2025, encompassing Fibrebond ($1.4B), Resilient Power, Ultra PCS ($1.55B), and the announced Boyd Thermal ($9.5B, pending). The Boyd deal represents Eaton's aggressive push into data center thermal management — the largest acquisition since Cooper Industries in 2012.

## 3. Organic vs. Inorganic Growth Breakdown

**FY2025 Total Growth: +10% YoY**
- **Organic: +8%** (the dominant contributor)
- **Acquisitions: +2%** (Fibrebond and smaller bolt-ons)
- **FX: ~0%** (roughly neutral)

**Historical Pattern**: Eaton is primarily an organic growth story punctuated by transformative M&A. The Cooper Industries deal (2012) was the defining transformation, converting Eaton from a diversified industrial into a power management company. Since then, M&A has been predominantly bolt-on ($0.5-1.5B range), focused on technology/capability adjacencies rather than revenue scaling. The Boyd Thermal deal ($9.5B) breaks this pattern — it is the first mega-acquisition since Cooper.

**2026 Guidance**: 7-9% organic growth, with inorganic contribution increasing as Boyd Thermal closes.

## 4. Management's Capital Allocation Priorities

Based on Q4 2025 earnings call and recent announcements:

1. **M&A Pipeline** — Focus on software, protected electrics, data center adjacencies (thermal/cooling), and aerospace bolt-ons. The $9.5B Boyd Thermal deal signals willingness to pay premium multiples for data center exposure.
2. **Capacity Expansion** — $1.5B deployed across ~24 projects to support secular demand from data centers, utilities, reshoring. Management cited a $3 trillion mega project pipeline.
3. **Mobility Spin-Off** — Vehicle + eMobility segments to be separated into independent public company by Q1 2027 (tax-free to shareholders). Post-spin, Eaton becomes a pure-play Electrical + Aerospace company.
4. **Dividends** — Continued priority; current annual dividend $4.16/share (~1.1% yield).
5. **Share Repurchases** — Suspended in 2026 to preserve capital for Boyd Thermal acquisition.

**Post-Spin Strategic Vision**: Management explicitly described the spin as enabling "sharper focus on leading businesses" — Electrical and Aerospace segments generated $24B in 2025 revenue at ~26% margins. The Mobility business ($3B at ~13% margins) was a margin drag and growth detractor.

## 5. Goodwill & Intangibles

| **Goodwill** | $15.77B | $14.71B | +$1.06B (+7.2%) |

| **Total Goodwill + Intangibles** | **$20.82B** | **$19.37B** | +$1.45B (+7.5%) |
| **Total Assets** | $41.25B | $38.38B | +$2.87B |
| **Goodwill / Total Assets** | **38.2%** | **38.3%** | Stable |
| **Goodwill + Intangibles / Total Assets** | **50.5%** | **50.5%** | Stable |

The $1.06B increase in goodwill from 2024 to 2025 primarily reflects the Fibrebond ($1.4B total price) and other 2025 bolt-on acquisitions. The Boyd Thermal acquisition ($9.5B, pending) will add significant additional goodwill when closed — likely $6-8B based on typical purchase price allocation for industrial acquisitions, which would push total goodwill above $22B.

### Goodwill by Segment (Approximate, from 10-K disclosures)
The 10-K indicates reporting units align with operating segments (except Aerospace, which has two reporting units). The bulk of goodwill (~$10-11B est.) resides in the two Electrical segments, primarily from the 2012 Cooper Industries acquisition. Vehicle and eMobility carry a smaller allocation and were tested qualitatively for impairment in 2024, suggesting their goodwill balances may be at risk if performance continues to deteriorate — though the pending spin-off resolves this concern for Eaton shareholders.
