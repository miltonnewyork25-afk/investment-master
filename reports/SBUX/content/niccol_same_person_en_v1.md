# The Same Person

Chipotle, 12:30 on a Tuesday.

Guy in front of me had his phone pinched between his ear and shoulder, mid-conversation. Didn't even look at the counter. Just said "chicken bowl" and kept talking.

I watched the line worker build it. White rice, black beans, chicken, salsa, sour cream, cheese, lettuce. One scoop after another, left to right, never stopped moving. By the time I'd decided between mild and hot, his bowl was at the register.

Under two minutes.

I ordered the same thing. Watched mine travel the same path. Same speed. Everyone at their station, adding their one thing. Nobody waiting on anyone else to finish.

I checked my phone on the way out. Four minutes since I'd walked in. Including the line.

---

Two days later. Starbucks drive-thru.

Two baristas behind the bar. One on the espresso machine—fast hands, but a small pause between each drink, eyes on the screen reading customizations. The other stickering mobile order bags. Their paths kept crossing. At one point both reached for the same cup.

One hand pulled back.

I watched for a while. The barista on the machine never moved. Grinder in front of her, cup in her hand, milk to her left, syrups to her right. She stayed in the same spot from the first drink to the last.

At Chipotle, a bowl moves down a line.

At Starbucks, a person stands in one place and turns.

---

These two companies were run by the same person. One after the other.

Brian Niccol took over Chipotle in 2018. The brand was nearly dead—food safety crisis, customers gone. Six and a half years later: order time down from twelve minutes to eight, margins from four points to nearly seventeen, stock up roughly sevenfold.

In August 2024, he left for Starbucks.

That day, Chipotle dropped seven and a half percent. About $25 billion in market cap, gone. Same day, Starbucks jumped nearly 25%. $50 billion appeared from nowhere.

One person changed offices. $75 billion rearranged itself around him.

---

What happened next is the interesting part.

After Niccol left, Chipotle was handed to his number two, Boatwright. First year: same-store sales went negative. From +11% under Niccol to -4% without him. The P/E compressed from 54 to 32.

If Chipotle's success was the system—the menu, the Chipotlanes, the digital infrastructure—then it should have held up with a different driver.

It didn't.

That's the natural experiment. The market wasn't pricing a system. It was pricing a person.

---

Here's the question.

If his value is personal—instinct, judgment, tempo—does that travel?

At Chipotle he faced an assembly line. Customers move along a counter, workers add ingredients in sequence. Linear. Parallelizable. He added a second line for digital orders. Throughput nearly doubled. Disciplined prep, tighter menu, better equipment. Every move accelerated a straight line.

At Starbucks he faces a single point. One barista standing at one machine. Grind, tamp, extract, steam, pour. Serial. You can't extract and steam at the same time—same machine. Adding a line doesn't help when the bottleneck isn't the line. It's the machine.

Our team estimated that roughly half of what he built at Chipotle can transfer to Starbucks. The other half has to be invented from scratch. That half happens to be the expensive one.

---

He's been there eighteen months.

Menu simplification under halfway done. The four-minute target hitting in some stores. Operating margin still under ten points. Six years ago it was sixteen.

At Chipotle, eighteen months in, margins had already climbed from four to eight.

Same person. Different pace.

---

Starbucks trades at 82 times earnings. Chipotle, without him, trades at 32.

What is 82 times paying for?

The bet that he can do inside a serial system what he did on an assembly line. That the half that doesn't transfer, he'll invent from scratch.

Maybe he can. He's done things before that looked impossible.

But Chipotle gave him something Starbucks can't—a straight line he could make faster.

---

I don't know if he'll pull it off. Our team hasn't agreed either.

I just keep seeing two pictures. A bowl moving down a counter in under two minutes. A barista standing in the same spot, drink after drink, going nowhere.

One line already proved it can't work without him. The other hasn't proved it can work with him.
