## 6. Acquisition Premium Analysis

### 6.1 Who Could Buy Intuit?

| Potential Acquirer | Strategic Logic | Feasibility |
|-------------------|----------------|-------------|
| **Microsoft** | QuickBooks + Dynamics 365 = SMB dominance; TurboTax + LinkedIn | **1994 attempt blocked by DOJ**. At $127B MCap, would need ~$160-180B deal. MSFT has ~$78B cash. Regulatory near-impossible (anti-trust) |
| **Oracle** | Add SMB to enterprise suite; compete with SAP | Oracle MCap ~$400B, could stretch financially but poor strategic fit. Larry Ellison has done mega-deals (Cerner $28B) but $170B+ would be unprecedented |
| **Salesforce** | CRM + SMB financial suite | CRM MCap ~$250B, significant overlap in SMB. DOJ would scrutinize |
| **Private Equity** | KKR/Apollo mega-LBO | At $127B, would need ~$55B equity + $100B+ debt at current rates. LBO math: $6.1B FCF → ~5.5% cash yield on $110B TEV. Debt service impossible at scale. **Not feasible.** |
| **Alphabet/Google** | Consumer fintech play (Credit Karma + Google Pay) | Regulatory dead-on-arrival given Google's existing antitrust issues |

### 6.2 LBO Math

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| TEV (current) | ~$131B |
| TTM FCF | ~$6.1B (FY2025); ~$7.9B est. TTM |
| FCF yield on TEV | ~4.7% (FY2025) to ~6.0% (TTM) |
| Required equity (40%) | ~$52B |
| Required debt (60%) | ~$79B |
| Interest cost @ 6% | ~$4.7B/year |
| FCF after debt service | ~$1.4B - $3.2B |
| Equity IRR (5Y exit @ 20x EBITDA) | ~8-12% — **below PE hurdle rate** |

**Verdict**: INTU is effectively **too big to acquire**. At $127B market cap (implying ~$150-170B with control premium), no single buyer can feasibly execute:
- Strategic: Anti-trust blocks the most logical buyers (MSFT, GOOG)
- PE: FCF yield too low relative to required leverage — LBO math doesn't work
- The 1994 Microsoft/DOJ precedent adds another layer of regulatory deterrence

### 6.3 Takeaway for Valuation

The absence of acquisition premium in the stock is rational — INTU trades purely on intrinsic business value, not M&A optionality. This is neither positive nor negative; it simply means the valuation floor is set by fundamentals, not a takeout bid.
