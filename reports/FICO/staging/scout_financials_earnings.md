## 4. FICO Score Pricing & Competitive Dynamics (Critical Risk Factor)

### 4.1 Mortgage Score Pricing History

| Period | Price Per Score | Change |
|--------|----------------|--------|
| Pre-2023 | $0.50 | — |
| 2023 | $2.10 | +320% |
| 2024 | $3.50 | +67% |
| 2025 | $4.95 | +41% |
| 2026 | **$10.00** | **+102%** |

**Key Development**: FICO announced a 2x price increase for 2026, raising the per-score fee from $4.95 to $10.00 for tri-merge resellers. Additionally, FICO launched a "Mortgage Direct License Program" where it sells scores directly to mortgage resellers, with a $4.95 royalty fee per score plus a $33 fee per borrower on funded loans.

### 4.2 Competitive & Regulatory Response

- **Equifax Response**: Equifax publicly condemned the "monopolistic" price increase and slashed its VantageScore 4.0 pricing to $4.50 per score (50%+ below FICO) for two years.
- **TransUnion**: Also engaged in the credit-score price war, positioning VantageScore as an alternative.
- **FHFA (Federal Housing Finance Agency)**: Director Bill Pulte directed Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to include VantageScore 4.0 alongside FICO scores, introducing competition into the GSE mortgage market for the first time.
- **Mortgage Bankers Association**: Criticized FICO's pricing as exploiting "oligopoly powers" during a period of constrained housing affordability.

**Assessment**: The 2026 price doubling is the most aggressive pricing action in FICO's history and has triggered meaningful regulatory and competitive pushback. While FICO's scores remain deeply embedded in lending workflows (switching costs are very high), the FHFA's decision to welcome VantageScore is a structural shift that could erode FICO's monopoly position over a 3-5 year horizon. This is the single biggest risk to the Scores business long-term trajectory.

## 9. Key Questions for Deep-Dive Investigation

1. **Pricing Sustainability**: Can FICO sustain the $10/score pricing given FHFA's decision to welcome VantageScore? What is the timeline for VantageScore adoption in GSE underwriting, and how does this affect FICO's pricing power?

2. **Regulatory Risk**: Is there meaningful risk of legislative or CFPB action to cap credit score pricing? What precedent exists?

3. **Platform Economics**: What is the true unit economics of FICO Platform vs. legacy products? As Platform mix rises, does this change the margin trajectory of the Software segment?

4. **Leverage Ceiling**: With net debt/EBITDA at 3.1x and rising, what is FICO's practical leverage limit? At what point does rating agency concern constrain the buyback machine?

5. **Mortgage Volume Sensitivity**: What % of Scores revenue comes from mortgage-related scores vs. auto/card/other? How sensitive is revenue to mortgage origination volumes independent of price?

6. **B2C Optionality**: myFICO.com and the direct-to-consumer business is growing ~8%. Is there a scenario where FICO builds a meaningful consumer fintech franchise?

7. **VantageScore Threat Assessment**: VantageScore is owned by the three bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion). With FHFA endorsement, what is the realistic 5-year share loss scenario for FICO in mortgage? In non-mortgage?

8. **Management Incentives**: CEO Will Lansing has been in role since 2012. What are his compensation incentives? Is the buyback strategy aligned with long-term value creation or short-term EPS optimization?

## 10. Preliminary Financial Model Anchors

### 10.1 Revenue Build Framework

**Scores Revenue Drivers**:
- Mortgage origination scores: Price ($10 in FY2026 vs. $4.95) x Volume (cyclical, tied to rates)
- Non-mortgage B2B scores: Auto, card, personal loan origination volumes x price
- B2C (myFICO): Subscription revenue, growing ~8-10%

**Software Revenue Drivers**:
- Platform ARR: Growing 30%+, currently $303M
- Non-Platform ARR: Declining ~8%, currently $463M
- Crossover point (Platform > Non-Platform): Likely FY2028 at current trends
- Professional services & license: Lumpy, declining

### 10.2 Implied FY2026 Quarterly Cadence

With FY2026 guidance of $2.35B (management) to $2.49B (consensus), and Q1 FY2026 at $512M:
- Q1 actual: $512M (run rate: $2.05B annualized)
- Remaining 3 quarters need: $1.84B to $1.97B ($613-$658M/quarter avg)
- This implies significant acceleration in Q2-Q4, likely driven by the $10 mortgage score pricing taking effect

### 10.3 FCF Power

At FY2026 consensus revenue of ~$2.5B and assuming:
- Operating margin: 47-49% (expanding from 46.5%)
- CapEx: ~$10-15M (capital-light)
- **Implied FY2026 FCF**: ~$950M-$1.0B
