## 1. DOJ Antitrust Investigation (2020) — Closed Without Action

- **March 15, 2020**: DOJ Antitrust Division opened a civil investigation into potential exclusionary conduct by FICO.
- **December 9, 2020**: DOJ closed the investigation. No enforcement action taken.
- **Significance**: FICO survived its first major federal antitrust probe, establishing a precedent that its market position (90% B2B credit scoring share) does not automatically constitute actionable monopoly abuse.
- **Source**: [FICO press release](https://www.fico.com/en/newsroom/fico-announces-doj-antitrust-investigation-closed)

## 2. In re FICO Antitrust Litigation (2020-Present) — Active, Surviving Dismissal

### Case Overview
- **Venue**: U.S. District Court, Northern District of Illinois
- **Judge**: Edmond E. Chang
- **Cases**: At least 10 related antitrust class action lawsuits filed since 2020
- **Plaintiffs**: Business-to-business purchasers of FICO scores (lenders, not consumers)
- **Defendants**: FICO + Equifax, Experian, TransUnion

### Core Allegations
1. FICO maintains monopoly power (~90% market share) through anticompetitive agreements with the three credit bureaus
2. Agreements contained clauses **preventing credit bureaus from marketing non-FICO scores** (e.g., VantageScore)
3. FICO penalized customers who sought both FICO and VantageScore scores
4. Plaintiffs were overcharged for credit scores as a result of suppressed competition

### Key Rulings
- **November 2024**: Judge Chang denied FICO's motion to dismiss on Sherman Act monopolization and state law claims. Ruling:
  - Sherman Act monopolization claims **can proceed** against FICO
  - No evidence the three credit bureaus **conspired** with FICO — conspiracy claims against bureaus dismissed with prejudice
  - Credit score buyers sufficiently backed antitrust claims against FICO alone
- **Credit bureaus freed**: VantageScore's separate antitrust suit against the bureaus was also resolved (bureaus freed)

### Risk Assessment
- **Litigation is alive and advancing**. FICO failed to kill the case at the motion-to-dismiss stage.
- Class certification has not yet been ruled on (as of March 2026 research). If granted, exposure could be substantial given FICO's pricing across the entire mortgage origination market.
- Potential damages: If FICO's ~500% price increases (2022-2025) are found to result from monopolistic conduct, treble damages under Sherman Act could be material.

## 3. Senator Josh Hawley's DOJ Investigation Calls (2024-2025)

### Timeline
- **2024 (Biden era)**: Hawley first called on DOJ Antitrust Division to investigate FICO's anticompetitive practices. Biden DOJ took no action.
- **April 3, 2025 (Trump era)**: Hawley **renewed** his call in a letter to Trump's DOJ Antitrust Division, citing:
  - FICO's ~90% market share in B2B credit scoring
  - Price hike from $3.50 to $4.95/score (41% increase) for mortgage originations
  - Government-mandated use: FHA and VA loans **require** FICO scores, creating a captive market
  - Burden falls disproportionately on lower-income borrowers

### Political Dynamics
- Hawley is a **Republican** senator and former state attorney general — this is bipartisan regulatory risk
- Other politicians and mortgage industry stakeholders have echoed the call
- **No public DOJ action taken** as of March 2026
- Trump DOJ is generally deregulatory, but Hawley's populist antitrust stance could generate pressure

### Significance for FICO
- Even without DOJ action, congressional attention keeps the pricing issue in public discourse
- Creates political cover for FHFA to accelerate VantageScore adoption as a competitive alternative
- Hawley's framing — "government-granted monopoly" — is politically potent across party lines

## 4. FICO Price Increase History (Critical Context)

## 5. CFPB Actions Under Biden Administration (2023-2024)

### Director Chopra's Anti-Monopoly Rhetoric
- **May 2024 (MBA speech)**: Chopra called out "price gouging" in credit reporting, specifically naming FICO's 400% increases
- Stated CFPB is "eager to hear from lenders" and will "look at possible rulemaking and guidance to improve competition, choice, and affordability"
- Referenced FICO's monopoly as a "cautionary lesson"
- Directed supervised institutions to test credit scoring models for prohibited basis disparities

### Credit Reporting Rulemaking Initiatives
1. **Medical Debt Rule (June 2024 proposed, January 2025 finalized)**: Would remove medical bills from credit reports. Over 70,000 comments received. **Vacated July 11, 2025** by U.S. District Court (E.D. Texas) after Trump CFPB joined plaintiffs in requesting vacatur.

2. **Data Broker Rule (December 3, 2024 proposed)**: Would classify data brokers selling credit scores, credit history, debt payments, or income data as consumer reporting agencies under FCRA. Comments due March 3, 2025. **Withdrawn May 15, 2025** by Trump CFPB.

3. **Fair Lending Supervision**: CFPB directed institutions to consider "less discriminatory" credit scoring models — indirect pressure on FICO's proprietary algorithms.

### Impact Assessment
- Biden-era CFPB was the most aggressive regulatory threat to FICO's pricing power
- Chopra's rhetoric created headline risk but no binding rules specifically constraining FICO pricing
- All major rulemaking initiatives were either vacated or withdrawn after Trump took office

## 6. Trump Administration and CFPB Dismantlement (2025-2026)

### Operational Gutting
- **February 2025**: Acting Director Russell Vought ordered CFPB office closure, instructed staff to "perform no work tasks"
- **Staff reduction**: Planned 88% workforce cut (1,700 → ~200 employees), including 90% of supervision and 80% of enforcement division
- **Enforcement collapse**: 16 of 34 ongoing enforcement actions dismissed with prejudice, 1 without prejudice
- **Guidance purge**: 67 guidance documents withdrawn in May 2025

### Funding Cuts
- **July 4, 2025**: "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" halved CFPB's statutory funding cap from 12% to 6.5% of Federal Reserve's 2009 operating expenses
- **December 30, 2025**: Federal judge ordered Trump administration must continue funding CFPB despite attempts to defund
- **January 2026**: NPR reported on CFPB's "turbulent year" — agency at existential risk

### Deregulatory Actions Relevant to FICO
1. **April 23, 2025**: Trump EO eliminated disparate-impact liability "to the maximum degree possible" — removes fair lending pressure on credit scoring
2. **May 2025**: Withdrew data broker proposed rule and 67 other guidance documents
3. **July 2025**: Joined plaintiffs to vacate medical debt credit reporting rule
4. **October 2025**: New FCRA interpretive rule broadened federal preemption of state credit reporting laws — **blocks states from imposing their own credit scoring regulations**

### Net Effect on FICO
**The regulatory threat from CFPB has collapsed.** The agency that was most actively scrutinizing FICO's pricing power is now functionally disabled. This is unambiguously positive for FICO's near-term pricing freedom.

## 7. FHFA Credit Score Transition (The Real Competitive Threat)

### Timeline
- **October 2022**: FHFA validated and approved FICO 10T and VantageScore 4.0 for GSE use
- **July 8, 2025**: FHFA announced lenders can soon use VantageScore 4.0 OR Classic FICO for Fannie/Freddie loans
- **Q4 2025 (target)**: Transition to new credit score models — subsequently delayed to TBD
- **November 2025**: FHFA nearing deal with FICO on FICO 10T historical data release
- **December 2025**: FICO agreed to publish 10T historical score data, removing final implementation barrier
- **Bi-merge transition**: Moving from tri-merge (all 3 bureaus) to bi-merge (2 bureaus) — also delayed to TBD

### VantageScore Growth
- 41.7 billion VantageScore credit scores used in 2024, up **55%** from 26.9 billion in 2023
- VantageScore 4.0 uses alternative data (rent, utilities, cell phone payments) and needs only 1 month of credit history vs. FICO's 6 months
- For the first time, VantageScore will be a **GSE-approved alternative** to FICO in mortgage originations

### Impact Assessment
- **This is the most material competitive threat to FICO's mortgage scoring monopoly** — more dangerous than any regulatory action
- FHFA mandating dual-score capability means lenders will have a choice for the first time
- However: FICO's brand entrenchment and lender inertia provide substantial switching cost moat
- FICO 10T adoption also benefits FICO — the transition forces lenders onto FICO's newest (and presumably higher-priced) model

## 8. State-Level Regulatory Actions

### Federal Preemption Strengthened
- **October 28, 2025**: Trump CFPB issued new interpretive rule asserting FCRA has **broad preemption power** over state credit reporting laws
- This replaced a 2022 Biden-era rule that interpreted preemption narrowly
- **Net effect**: States are now largely blocked from imposing their own credit scoring regulations

### State Medical Debt Protections
- Multiple states advancing their own medical debt credit reporting protections after federal rule was vacated
- These affect credit bureau reporting practices but do not directly regulate FICO's scoring methodology or pricing

### Assessment
- State-level regulatory risk to FICO is **minimal** under current federal preemption interpretation
- Even activist state AGs would face preemption challenges if targeting credit scoring practices

## 12. Key Conclusions for FICO Investment Thesis

### The Regulatory Paradox
FICO faces a paradox: the political rhetoric against its monopoly is bipartisan and intensifying (Hawley, Chopra, MBA), but the actual regulatory apparatus capable of constraining its pricing has been dismantled. The CFPB — the only federal agency with direct consumer finance rulemaking authority — is functionally inoperative. DOJ has investigated and closed without action. No legislation has advanced.

### What Actually Threatens Pricing Power
1. **Private antitrust litigation** (In re FICO) — the only active legal constraint proceeding through courts
2. **FHFA-mandated VantageScore adoption** — introduces genuine competition in FICO's most profitable channel (mortgage originations) for the first time
3. **Market backlash / lender defection** — if FICO pushes pricing too aggressively, it accelerates VantageScore adoption

### What Doesn't (Currently) Threaten Pricing Power
1. CFPB rulemaking — agency gutted
2. DOJ investigation — no appetite under Trump
3. Congressional legislation — noise, not action
4. State regulation — preempted

### Time Horizon Consideration
- **Near-term (2026-2027)**: Regulatory risk is at a multi-year low. FICO has maximum pricing freedom.
- **Medium-term (2028+)**: If Democrats regain the White House, CFPB reconstitution could reignite regulatory pressure. The In re FICO litigation could reach class certification or settlement. VantageScore adoption in GSE channels could erode share.
- **Long-term**: The structural question is whether FHFA's dual-score mandate breaks FICO's mortgage monopoly, or whether brand inertia and switching costs preserve it. Historical precedent (FICO has maintained dominance for 30+ years despite periodic competitive threats) favors the incumbent.
