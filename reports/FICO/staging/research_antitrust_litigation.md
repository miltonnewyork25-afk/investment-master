## 1. Class-Action Lawsuits in N.D. Illinois

### Case: *In re FICO Antitrust Litigation*, N.D. Ill. (Case No. 1:20-cv-02559)

**Judge**: Edmond E. Chang, U.S. District Court, Northern District of Illinois

**Number of Cases**: At least 10 antitrust class-action lawsuits filed between 2020-2023, consolidated under Judge Chang.

**Plaintiffs**: Credit unions (incl. Sky Federal Credit Union), banks, mortgage lenders, real estate brokerages, auto dealers, and other businesses that purchase FICO scores (B2B purchasers).

**Structure**: Court separated indirect-purchaser plaintiffs from direct-purchaser plaintiffs. Scott+Scott appointed sole lead counsel for the direct-purchaser class; MoloLamken also involved for local presence.

### Key Allegations

1. **Monopoly maintenance** — FICO maintains ~90% market share in B2B credit scoring through anticompetitive agreements, meritless litigation, and exclusionary practices
2. **Artificially inflated pricing** — FICO charges monopoly rents that would not be possible in a competitive market
3. **Anticompetitive contract clauses** — Agreements with credit bureaus contain clauses that:
   - Prevent bureaus from marketing non-FICO credit scores
   - Penalize customers who seek both FICO and VantageScore
   - Include "No Equivalent Product" restrictions (see Section 5)
   - Include punitive royalty structures (see Section 5)
4. **Meritless litigation** — FICO filed suit against credit bureaus and VantageScore in 2006, allegedly intended to drive VantageScore out of business

### Procedural Status

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| 2020-2023 | 10+ lawsuits filed, consolidated in N.D. Illinois |
| Sept 2023 | Judge Chang denies FICO's motion to dismiss — rules plaintiffs presented enough evidence that FICO violated antitrust laws; cases proceed |
| Nov 2024 | Judge Chang rules Sherman Act monopolization claims + state law claims against FICO can proceed; remaining claims against FICO and the three major credit bureaus dismissed with prejudice |
| As of Mar 2026 | Cases in discovery phase; class certification motion not yet ruled on (based on available public information) |

**Key Ruling Language**: Judge Chang found plaintiffs presented "enough early evidence to pursue allegations that FICO violated antitrust laws" through monopolistic practices that restrain competition in the credit scoring market.

## 2. DOJ Investigation History

### Timeline

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| March 13, 2020 | DOJ Antitrust Division opens civil investigation into "potential exclusionary conduct by FICO" |
| Dec 2020 | DOJ informs FICO the investigation is closed; no enforcement action taken |
| April 3, 2025 | Sen. Hawley sends letter to DOJ urging reopening of antitrust investigation |
| As of Mar 2026 | No publicly confirmed reopening of DOJ investigation |

### Assessment

The 2020 investigation lasted ~9 months and was closed without action during the first Trump administration. The investigation was triggered by ongoing TransUnion-FICO litigation and the filing of the class-action suits. The 2025 Hawley letter to the second Trump DOJ specifically referenced FICO's 90% market share and 40%+ price increases. CHLA and other industry groups have echoed the call for DOJ investigation.

**Risk Factor**: A reopened DOJ investigation would represent a material escalation. The political dynamics are unusual — bipartisan concern (Hawley R-Mo. leading) and industry-wide opposition from mortgage lenders creates potential for action regardless of administration.

## 5. Anticompetitive Contract Clauses

### "7x Penalty Royalty" (Pre-Qualification Penalty)

FICO implemented a "Pre-Qualification" royalty category that imposed a **seven-times (7x) penalty rate** on lenders that purchased a FICO Score for use in "Pre-Qualification" alongside VantageScore (or any other competing credit score).

**Mechanism**: If a lender used FICO for underwriting but also used VantageScore for pre-qualification, the lender would be charged 7x the normal royalty for the FICO score.

**Alleged Purpose**: Plaintiffs allege this penalty was "intended to prevent, and did in fact prevent, lenders from relying on additional or alternative credit scores." Effectively a tying/exclusive dealing mechanism enforced through pricing rather than contractual prohibition.

### "No Equivalent Product" Clause

FICO's contracts with credit bureaus include a provision that **prohibits a credit bureau from developing or distributing a non-FICO analytic product** (i.e., a competing credit score).

**Implications**: This clause allegedly prevented Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion from independently developing or actively marketing VantageScore or any other scoring alternative while under contract with FICO.

### Combined Effect (Plaintiffs' Theory)

The contract structure creates a two-layer lock-in:
1. **Supply-side lock** — Bureaus cannot develop/distribute competing scores ("No Equivalent Product")
2. **Demand-side lock** — Lenders face 7x penalty for using competing scores alongside FICO

This allegedly creates a classic monopoly maintenance mechanism: even after VantageScore was developed (jointly by the three bureaus), FICO's contractual restrictions prevented meaningful distribution and adoption.

## 6. Treble Damages Calculation Framework

### Legal Basis

Under the Clayton Act (15 U.S.C. Section 15), plaintiffs who prove violations of federal antitrust laws are entitled to **automatic treble damages** plus prejudgment interest and costs. No special showing of "egregious conduct" is required — only violation and some damages need be proven.

### Overcharge Estimation Framework

**FICO Scores Segment Revenue** (for scale):
- FY2024 (ending Sept 2024): ~$919M Scores revenue (Q4 alone = $249.2M)
- FY2025 (ending Sept 2025): $1.169B Scores revenue (Q4 = $311.6M, +25% YoY)
- B2B revenue growth driven "largely by higher unit prices" (not volume)

**Price Escalation Timeline** (mortgage origination royalty per score):
| Period | Price | Change |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Pre-2018 | ~$0.50-0.60 | Baseline |
| 2023 | $0.60-$2.75 (tiered) | Up to 400% for some lenders |
| 2024 | $3.50 (flat) | Simplified after backlash |
| 2025 | $4.95 | +41% |
| 2026 (proposed) | ~$10.00 | +102% |

**Illustrative Damages Calculation** (analyst estimate, NOT court-determined):

If the "but-for" competitive price is assumed to be ~$0.60-$1.00 (pre-monopoly-pricing level), and FICO generated cumulative Scores B2B revenue of roughly $3-4B over the class period (2020-2025), then:
- Overcharge = (Actual price - Competitive price) / Actual price x Revenue
- If overcharge is 50-70% of revenue: ~$1.5-2.8B in single damages
- **Treble damages: ~$4.5-8.4B**
- Plus prejudgment interest and attorney fees

**Important Caveats**:
- Class has NOT been certified yet; damages class may differ from liability class
- Settlement typically occurs at a fraction of treble damages (academic research suggests most price-fixing class actions settle for less than single damages)
- FICO's total market cap is ~$40-50B — treble damages at the high end would be material but not existential
- The "but-for" competitive price is heavily contested and would require expert testimony

## 7. FHFA Actions — Thompson and Pulte

### Under Director Sandra Thompson

| Date | Action |
|------|--------|
| Oct 2022 | FHFA validates FICO 10T and VantageScore 4.0 for use by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac |
| 2023-2024 | FHFA releases historical VantageScore 4.0 credit scores on "tens of millions of loans" for market preparation |
| 2024 | Announces move from tri-merge to bi-merge credit reporting requirement |

**Thompson Quote**: "The release of historical credit scores on tens of millions of loans provides an extensive resource to help market participants prepare for this transition... The use of these modernized credit score models will enhance risk management while furthering sustainable access to credit for consumers."

**Key Policy**: Thompson's FHFA implemented the Credit Score Competition Act of 2018, which required FHFA to establish a validation process for multiple credit scoring models. This was the regulatory foundation for breaking FICO's GSE monopoly.

### Under Director William Pulte (Trump 2.0 Appointee)

| Date | Action |
|------|--------|
| July 8, 2025 | Announces lenders can use VantageScore 4.0 OR Classic FICO (lender choice) for Fannie/Freddie loans |
| H2 2025 | Final approval of VantageScore 4.0 for immediate use in conforming mortgage market |
| Nov 2025 | FHFA nearing deal for new FICO credit scoring model (FICO 10T) for mortgages |

**Pulte's Stance**: CHLA "commended" and "supported" Director Pulte for his comments on FICO, indicating Pulte has been publicly critical of FICO's pricing. This represents rare bipartisan/cross-administration continuity on the FICO competition issue.

**Policy Impact**: The lender-choice model (VantageScore 4.0 OR Classic FICO) is the **single most material regulatory threat** to FICO's Scores segment pricing power. If VantageScore achieves meaningful GSE-market share, FICO loses the pricing leverage that comes from being the sole mandated score.

**VantageScore Adoption Data**: Online usage of VantageScore mortgage scores rose 74% YoY in H1 2025. However, questions remain about lender readiness to fully switch.

## 8. State-Level Regulatory Actions

### Illinois Attorney General

**CHLA Letter (October 9, 2024)**: Community Home Lenders of America sent a formal letter to Illinois AG Elizabeth Maxeiner requesting an antitrust investigation into FICO's practices. Key points:
- FICO's 90% market share constitutes anticompetitive dominance
- 400% price increases in 24 months
- Disproportionate harm to "low-income, minority, and other underserved populations"
- Practices "hindering competition, stifling innovation, and ultimately harming consumers"

**Status**: No publicly confirmed investigation opened by Illinois AG as of research date.

### Broader State AG Landscape

State attorneys general are increasingly focused on algorithmic pricing and housing-market antitrust issues (e.g., RealPage investigations). FICO's pricing practices fit the emerging state AG focus on housing affordability. However, **no state AG has publicly opened a FICO-specific investigation** as of March 2026.

**Risk Assessment**: State AG action is a MODERATE future risk. The CHLA letter provides a template for multi-state advocacy. If the Illinois AG acts, other states could follow. The "harm to underserved communities" framing is particularly potent for state AG offices.

## 9. MBA (Mortgage Bankers Association) Advocacy

### Position

MBA has been the most vocal industry opponent of FICO's pricing practices.

**CEO Bob Broeksmit**: "Once again, the national credit bureaus are abusing their government-granted oligopoly by gouging consumers."

### Key Advocacy Points

1. **Tri-merge to single-pull reform** — MBA calls for allowing single-repository credit reports instead of tri-merge (reduces FICO score purchases per loan from 3 to 1)
2. **DOJ investigation** — MBA explicitly calls for DOJ antitrust investigation
3. **CFPB action** — MBA calls for CFPB to "explore remedies including fee caps and interoperability requirements"
4. **Price transparency** — MBA decries "deeply flawed and anticompetitive market structure"

### Price Documentation by MBA

MBA reported in December 2025:
- 35-40% or more pricing increases proposed for 2026
- Fourth consecutive year of steep increases
- Credit report costs per closed loan: $50 (2022) → $150-200 (2024) → projected higher in 2025-2026
- Cumulative increase from $0.60 to $10.00 per score over ~5 years = **~1,567% increase**

### Coalition Building

MBA is not alone. Allied organizations include:
- **CHLA** (Community Home Lenders of America) — Letters to IL AG and Congress
- **NAMU** (National Association of Mortgage Underwriters) — Industry coverage
- **USMI** (U.S. Mortgage Insurers) — Supports Pulte's credit score modernization

## 10. Risk Taxonomy for Investment Analysis

### Litigation Risk Matrix

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Timing | Notes |
|------|------------|--------|--------|-------|
| Class certification granted | 40-55% | HIGH — enables settlement pressure | 2026-2027 | Judge Chang has been receptive to plaintiffs |
| Trial verdict for plaintiffs | 15-25% | VERY HIGH — treble damages | 2028+ | Most antitrust cases settle pre-trial |
| Settlement | 50-65% | MODERATE — likely single-digit % of revenue | 2026-2028 | Antitrust settlements typically < single damages |
| DOJ reopens investigation | 20-30% | HIGH — regulatory overhang | 2025-2026 | Hawley pressure + bipartisan concern |
| State AG investigation | 15-25% | MODERATE | 2025-2027 | CHLA template exists |
| CFPB fee cap regulation | 5-10% | VERY HIGH | Unlikely near-term | CFPB curtailed under Trump 2.0 |
| VantageScore GSE adoption >20% | 30-40% | VERY HIGH — structural pricing pressure | 2026-2028 | FHFA policy enables it; adoption TBD |

### Key Uncertainty: Pricing Power Durability

The central investment question is whether FICO's Scores pricing power is:
- **(A) Durable monopoly** — Network effects + regulatory mandate + switching costs protect 90% share and pricing regardless of litigation/regulation
- **(B) Eroding monopoly** — VantageScore GSE approval + litigation + political pressure create a 3-5 year window where pricing power degrades materially
- **(C) Disruption risk** — AI/alternative data models (e.g., Upstart, Zest AI) eventually commoditize traditional credit scoring

The antitrust litigation and regulatory actions documented above are primarily relevant to scenario (B).
