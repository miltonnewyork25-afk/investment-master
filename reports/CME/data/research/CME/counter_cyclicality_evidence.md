## 2. VIX Correlation Pattern

| VIX Range | Typical CME ADV Impact | Revenue Impact |
|---|---|---|
| <15 (calm) | Baseline ADV | Stable, modest growth |
| 15–25 (normal) | +5–15% above baseline | Normal growth |
| 25–35 (elevated) | +20–40% above baseline | Outsized revenue |
| 35+ (crisis) | +50–100%+ above baseline | Record-breaking |

Key pattern: **Asymmetric payoff structure**
- Vol spikes → outsized revenue gains (near-100% incremental margin)
- Vol compression → revenue declines modestly (fixed costs stable, not zero)
- The distribution is positively skewed for CME

## 3. CRITICAL: Business vs. Stock Counter-Cyclicality

### The Business IS Counter-Cyclical

| Crisis | CME Revenue | S&P 500 Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| 2008 GFC | +12% | -10% |
| 2020 COVID | +29% (Q1) | -15% (Q1) |
| 2022 Rate Hikes | +15% | Flat |
| 2025 Tariffs | +6.5% | TBD |

### The Stock IS NOT Counter-Cyclical

| Period | CME Stock | S&P 500 |
|---|---|---|
| 2008 (peak to trough) | **-70%** | -51% |
| Q1 2020 (Jan–Mar) | **-25%** | -20% |
| 2022 Full Year | **-8%** | -18% |

- **2008 is the critical data point**: CME stock fell 70% despite business performing well
- Why? Multiple compression from 35x → 12x P/E as growth premium evaporated
- CME was trading at peak multiples entering 2008 — financial crisis repriced all financial stocks regardless of fundamentals
- **Lesson**: Revenue counter-cyclicality does NOT guarantee stock counter-cyclicality, especially from elevated valuations

## 4. Revenue Asymmetry Quantification

| Scenario | Revenue Impact (est.) | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| VIX spike to 40+ | +25–40% quarterly revenue | 1–3 months |
| VIX normalization to <15 | -5–10% quarterly revenue | 6–12 months |
| Rate hike cycle (300bp+) | +15–25% annual revenue | 12–24 months |
| Rate stability (0bp change) | -3–5% annual revenue | Ongoing |

- **Upside capture >> downside risk** in revenue terms
- Calm markets don't destroy revenue — they just slow growth to low-single-digits
- Volatile markets produce outsized, high-margin revenue bursts
- This is the core of CME's economic model
