## 5. Interest Rate Sensitivity

### IORB Mechanism
- CME deposits cash collateral primarily at the **Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago**
- Earns the **Interest on Reserve Balances (IORB) rate** — currently ~4.40% (post-Dec 2025 cut)
- At Dec 2024: **$87.4B deposited at Fed Reserve Bank of Chicago** (per 10-K)
- Remainder invested in Treasuries, agency paper, money market

### Sensitivity Estimate
| Rate Change | Gross Impact ($M) | Net Impact ($M, est.) |
|---|---:|---:|
| +100bp | +$500–800M | +$50–120M |
| -100bp | -$500–800M | -$50–120M |
| -200bp | -$1,000–1,600M | -$100–240M |

- Gross impact is large but **most is passed through**
- Net impact depends on the spread CME retains
- In a zero-rate environment (2020–2021), this entire revenue stream effectively disappears

### CME Retains ~5–15bp Spread
- The spread between what CME earns (IORB) and what it pays to clearing members
- Exact spread not disclosed — estimated from net retention data
- Spread appears to be **widening** in 2025 (retention rate jumped to ~15.7%)

## 6. Cash Minimum & Compliance Rules

### 30% Soft Minimum Cash Rule
- CME requires clearing members to post a **minimum 30% of performance bonds in cash**
- Members posting <30% cash incur a **10bp administrative fee** on the shortfall
- This creates a cash floor that supports investment income even when members prefer non-cash collateral

### CFTC Regulation 1.25 Reinvestment Restrictions
- Limits what CME can invest customer segregated funds in
- **Permitted**: US Treasuries, agency securities, money market funds, Fed deposits, bank CDs, commercial paper (with restrictions)
- **Prohibited**: equities, corporate bonds, derivatives, structured products
- Effectively forces conservative, short-duration portfolio — minimal credit risk but capped yield

## 7. Key Insight: Hidden Revenue Stream

This investment income sits in **non-operating income** on the income statement, making it easy to overlook:

```
Operating Revenue: $6,520M    ← what most analysts focus on
Operating Income:  $4,455M
---
Investment Income (gross):  $5,737M    ← LARGER than operating revenue
Distributed to members:    ($4,837M)
Net retained:               $900M      ← 20% of operating income, sitting below the line
---
Net Income:                $3,655M
```

- The **gross** investment income ($5.7B) is actually **larger** than total operating revenue ($6.5B) — but it's mostly pass-through
- The net ~$275–900M is real economic value that accrues to CME shareholders
- This creates a **hidden interest rate sensitivity** in the stock that isn't captured in typical operating earnings models
- In a rate-cut cycle, this tailwind reverses — but volume tailwind from rate volatility may partially offset
