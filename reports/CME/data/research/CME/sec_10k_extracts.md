### 3.1 Gross Investment Income ($M)

| Item | FY2025 | FY2024 | YoY |

> Note: "Other non-operating expense" includes distribution of interest earned on clearing member collateral reinvestments. The exact split is [10-K NEEDED], but the bulk of "Other non-operating expense" represents pass-through interest to clearing members.

### 3.2 Performance Bond / Collateral Data
- **Total collateral held (Q2 2025 avg)**: ~$316B (up from $290B in Q1 2025)
- **Cash collateral (Q2 2025 avg)**: ~$133B (~48% of required amount, above 30% soft minimum)
- **Soft minimum cash requirement**: 30% of performance bond requirement (implemented April 2025)
- **Guaranty fund contributions**: ~$10.1B available to collateralize multi-currency revolving credit facility
- **Interest rate policy**: CME earns interest on reinvested cash collateral, distributes a portion back to clearing members at rates below the earned rate — the spread is CME's net investment income

### 3.3 Investment Income Economics
- CME acts as an intermediary: collects cash performance bonds → invests in short-term instruments → earns full return → pays clearing members a lower rate → retains the spread
- Gross investment income surged 41% ($4.1B→$5.7B) due to: (1) higher collateral balances from soft minimum requirement, (2) sustained high short-term interest rates
- **Key risk**: If rates fall, both gross income and the spread compress. This is a significant sensitivity for CME earnings.

## 5. Risk Factors — Key Disclosures

> Source: 10-K Item 1A (page 16+), supplemented by secondary analysis

### 5.1 Competition Risk
- **Increasing competition** from foreign and domestic entities, including new market entrants and industry consolidation
- Key competitors: ICE (Intercontinental Exchange), Cboe Global Markets, Eurex, SGX, HKEX
- Competition in OTC clearing from LCH (LSEG)
- FX: competition from EBS (CME-owned), Refinitiv, institutional ECNs
- **European benchmark licensing risk**: EU legislators may prohibit or restrict exclusive licenses for benchmark indexes, which could impact equity index product profitability

### 5.2 Regulatory Risk
- Substantial resources required for regulatory compliance (CFTC, SEC, international regulators)
- Changes in derivatives regulation could alter market structure
- **Cryptocurrency regulation**: Outcome of crypto regulation debate may impact existing offerings or ability to launch future products
- Cross-border regulatory fragmentation remains a risk
- Potential for mandatory clearing mandates (positive) or position limits (negative)

### 5.3 Technology Risk
- Must keep pace with rapid technological developments
- Capital expenditure required for platform modernization
- Cloud migration risk (see Google Cloud partnership below)
- **November 2025 outage**: 10-hour trading halt due to CyrusOne cooling system failure — exposed single-point-of-failure in data center infrastructure

### 5.4 Volume Dependency
- Revenue heavily dependent on trading volumes
- Volumes influenced by global economic/political conditions, market volatility, competition
- Reduction in trading activity would adversely impact financial performance

### 5.5 Cybersecurity (Item 1C, page 27)
- Technology infrastructure vulnerable to security risks
- Cyber attacks, data breaches, and operational disruptions identified as material risks

### 5.6 Credit/Counterparty Risk
- Clearing operations create counterparty risk concentrations
- Default of a major clearing member could require use of guaranty fund
- CME acts as central counterparty — systemically important financial market utility (SIFMU)

## 6. Customer Concentration & Clearing Membership

### 6.1 Clearing Member Count
- **Total clearing firms**: ~69 (per Maoxian.com clearing member directory, cross-referenced)
- **Member types**: CME/CBOT/NYMEX/COMEX四个交易所分别注册 + OTC IRS清算会员
- **Representative members**: ABN AMRO, Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan, Morgan Stanley, Wells Fargo等

### 6.2 Revenue Concentration
- CME does not publicly disclose the percentage of revenue from individual clearing members
- Clearing member base is concentrated among large global banks and proprietary trading firms
- Top clearing members include: JP Morgan, Goldman Sachs, Citadel Securities, Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, Barclays, etc.
- A significant portion of clearing fees comes from a relatively small number of large firms
- **Market structure**: ~69 clearing members account for the vast majority of volumes
- No single customer disclosed as >10% of revenue in prior filings (standard SEC threshold)

### 6.3 Default Waterfall Structure (Financial Safeguards)
1. **Layer 1**: 违约会员自身保证金(Performance Bond) + 违约基金贡献
2. **Layer 2**: CME Clearing自有出资 — Base产品$100M / IRS产品$150M
3. **Layer 3**: 非违约会员违约基金按比例使用
4. **Layer 4**: 非违约会员追加评估(Assessment Powers) — 上限为各非违约会员Base基金贡献的2.75倍(单次违约)
- **基金设计标准**: Cover 2 — 覆盖最大两家清算会员及关联方同时违约的潜在损失
- **历史违约**: 10-K及公开披露中未报告重大清算会员违约事件; 44年无违约记录(含2008金融危机)

### 6.4 Regulatory Designation
- **SIFMU**: Systemically Important Financial Market Utility(系统重要金融市场基础设施)
- **SIDCO**: Systemically Important Derivatives Clearing Organization(系统重要衍生品清算组织)
- 受CFTC(主要) + SEC + Federal Reserve Board三重监管

## 7. Related Party Transactions

### 7.1 S&P Dow Jones Indices LLC Joint Venture
- **CME Group ownership**: 27% (increased from initial 24.4% in 2013)
- **S&P Global ownership**: 73%
- **Accounting treatment**: Equity method — reported in "Equity in net earnings of unconsolidated subsidiaries"
- **FY2025 equity earnings**: $371.7M (vs $350.9M in FY2024, +5.9%)
- **Licensing arrangement**: CME pays S&P DJI a share of profits from equity index product complex (futures, options, swaps based on S&P/Dow Jones indexes)
- **Reverse flow**: CME earns 27% of S&P DJI net income through equity method
- **Licensing expense**: Included in "Licensing and other fee agreements" ($371.0M in FY2025 total, which also includes other licensing fees)
- **Exclusive licensing**: CME has exclusive rights to list futures/options on S&P 500, DJIA, and related indexes — a critical competitive moat

### 7.2 Google Cloud Partnership
- **Announced**: November 2021
- **Term**: 10-year strategic partnership
- **Google investment**: $1B in CME Group non-voting convertible preferred stock
- **Purpose**: Migrate CME's technology infrastructure to Google Cloud; build new cloud-native trading platform
- **Aurora facility**: New private Google Cloud region + co-location facility in Aurora, IL (announced June 2024)
- **FY2025 technology expenses related to cloud migration**: ~$115M (guidance)
- **Timeline**: CME expects to provide clients ≥18 months advance notice before markets move to new platform
- **Risk**: Cloud migration is multi-year, complex; any disruption during transition could impact trading

## 8. Commitments & Contingencies

### 8.1 CyrusOne Data Center Lease
- **Original transaction**: CME sold Aurora, IL data center to CyrusOne for $130M in 2016
- **Lease term**: 15-year sale-leaseback (expires ~2031)
- **Facility size**: ~428,000 sq ft originally, expanded to ~450,000 sq ft across 3 data centers on campus
- **Expansion**: CyrusOne broke ground on $350M new data center in Aurora (October 2024) — part of CME/Google Cloud collaboration
- **November 2025 incident**: 10-hour outage due to cooling system failure (human error — staff failed to follow cold-weather preparation protocols). Trading of US Treasury futures, gold, crude oil, FX halted globally. CyrusOne installed additional cooling redundancy post-incident.

### 8.2 Credit Facilities
- **Multi-currency revolving credit facility**: $2.3B maintained
- **Additional facility**: $350M
- **364-day multi-currency facility**: Available
- **Collateral**: Guaranteed by clearing member guaranty fund contributions (~$10.1B)

### 8.3 Long-Term Debt
- Total long-term debt: $3,422.3M (as of Dec 31, 2025)
- Essentially unchanged from $3,428.0M at Dec 31, 2024
- Interest/borrowing costs: $173.4M (FY2025)

### 8.4 Operating Lease Obligations [10-K NEEDED for full schedule]
- Right-of-use assets and corresponding lease liabilities recognized on balance sheet
- Key leases: CyrusOne Aurora facility, office space (Chicago HQ at 20 S. Wacker Drive, New York, London, other global offices)
- Exact maturity schedule not available from press release data

### 8.5 Other Commitments
- Purchase obligations for advertising, software, and contractual commitments
- Capital expenditures for 2026 technology investments planned (cloud migration)
- Holding company structure: relies on subsidiary dividends; covenants restrict operational flexibility
- Dividends paid in FY2025: $3.9B to shareholders
