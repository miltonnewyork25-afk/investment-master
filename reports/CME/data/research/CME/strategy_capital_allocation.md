## 2. Dividend Structure

### Quarterly Dividend
- Current: **~$1.15/share** per quarter ($4.60/year)
- Steady, predictable, growing ~5–8% annually
- Yield on quarterly alone: ~1.8% at $255/share

### Annual Variable Dividend (Paid in January)

| Year (Paid) | Variable Dividend ($/share) | YoY Change |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2020 | $2.50 | -38% |
| 2021 | $3.25 | +30% |
| 2022 | $3.60 | +11% |
| 2023 | $5.25 | +46% |
| 2024 | $5.80 | +10% |
| 2025 | $6.15 | +6% |

- Variable dividend = function of excess cash after CapEx, debt service, and quarterly dividends
- 2023 step-change: record revenue + investment income retention → outsized variable
- Total 2025 payout: $4.60 (quarterly) + $6.15 (variable) = **$10.75/share** → **4.2% yield**

### Cumulative Capital Return
- **$28B+ cumulative dividends** since 2012 annual variable program began
- No share buybacks until December 2024 — dividends were the sole return mechanism
- Philosophy: return ALL excess cash, don't hoard it

## 6. CME Securities Clearing — Treasury Clearing Mandate

| Milestone | Date |
|---|---|
| SEC approval for CME Securities Clearing | December 2025 |
| Expected launch | Q2 2026 |
| Treasury clearing mandate deadline | December 2026 (cash) / June 2027 (repo) |

- **SEC Rule 17Ad-22e** mandates central clearing for US Treasury cash and repo transactions
- Currently ~$700B/day in Treasuries cleared by FICC (DTCC subsidiary) — near-monopoly
- CME entering as second clearing option — leveraging BrokerTec platform + existing Treasury futures clearing
- **Cross-margining opportunity**: clearing both Treasury futures AND cash Treasuries → massive margin efficiency
- Potential revenue: $200–500M annually at maturity (2028+) based on volume capture estimates
- This is CME's biggest organic growth initiative since SOFR

## 8. Terry Duffy — CEO & Strategic Philosophy

| Aspect | Approach |
|---|---|
| Tenure | Chairman since 2002, Executive Chairman & CEO since 2016 |
| Core Philosophy | Narrow focus on derivatives, don't diversify |
| M&A Discipline | Acquisitive but selective — only derivatives-adjacent |
| Cloud Advocacy | Strong proponent of Google Cloud migration |
| Capital Return | Historically all-dividend, now adding buybacks |
| Public Stance | Vocal on regulation, free markets, competition with FICC |

### Strategic Principles
1. **Stay in lane**: Derivatives clearing and trading only — no banking, no insurance, no asset management
2. **Network effects > product innovation**: Liquidity begets liquidity — protect the network
3. **Regulation as moat**: Embrace regulation (CFTC, SEC) — compliance costs deter new entrants
4. **Incremental adjacencies**: Expand into related areas (Treasury clearing, event contracts) rather than unrelated ones
