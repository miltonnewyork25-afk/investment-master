### 8.2 Concentration Risk Factors

1. **VIE Structure**: BABA ADR holders do not own equity in Alibaba directly but in a Cayman Islands holding company with contractual rights to economic benefits. This limits institutional comfort for some allocators and is the primary reason for structurally low institutional ownership vs. US peers.

2. **Dual Listing Fragmentation**: Ownership data is fragmented across NYSE (ADR) and HKEX 9988.HK. True institutional ownership is materially higher than 14-15% when combining both markets.

3. **SoftBank Overhang (Largely Resolved)**: SoftBank's repeated sales between 2022-2024 created persistent overhang. The 2024-2025 data is ambiguous (possible data artifacts), but the structural overhang from SoftBank is much smaller than 2022-2023.

4. **Founder Alignment**: Ma (~4%) + Tsai (~1.4%) = ~5.4% combined. Both purchased aggressively at 2023 lows. Moderate insider ownership by mega-cap standards but meaningful given $370B+ market cap.

5. **Hedge Fund Turnover**: High-profile names (Burry, Bridgewater) showed extreme position volatility (3,360% increase followed by complete exit for Bridgewater). BABA remains a tactical/thematic trade for many hedge funds rather than a long-term conviction hold. Only Tepper (10 years) and Daily Journal (~4 years) demonstrate multi-year holding patterns.

## 9. Key Takeaways for Investment Analysis

1. **Institutional ownership is structurally understated** in US 13F data due to dual listing. True institutional ownership combining NYSE + HKEX is likely 40-45%, not the 14-15% shown in US-only data.

2. **Smart money is divided but net positive**: Tepper remains the highest-conviction famous holder (~$785M, 11% of portfolio) but has been trimming measured. New entry by Dan Loeb ($121M) provides a fresh bullish signal. Holding-steady positions (Daily Journal, ShawSpring, Chou, Causeway) add breadth. The Burry/Bridgewater exits were driven by fund-level factors, not BABA fundamentals.

3. **The buyback program is the strongest "insider" signal**: $11.9B repurchased in FY2025, $19.1B remaining through March 2027. This is management's most credible expression of undervaluation belief, though the execution pace has decelerated.

4. **Munger's cautionary tale remains relevant**: The "one of my worst mistakes" admission from a value investing legend highlights the VIE/governance/retail margin risks that persist even at seemingly cheap valuations. The remaining 195K shares are now held under inertia rather than active conviction.

5. **SoftBank's status requires clarification**: After the historical complete exit in 2024, the 2024-2025 re-entry data contains anomalies (2^31-1 share figure) that suggest data vendor artifacts rather than genuine re-entry. Treat SoftBank as a largely resolved overhang.

6. **Net Q4 2025 flow was positive**: Aggregate hedge fund shares increased 1.61%, with Third Point's new $121M position being the most notable addition. Tepper's reduction was measured rather than aggressive.

7. **Low short interest (2%) is a positive signal**: Institutional bears are not aggressively positioned against BABA, suggesting the risk/reward is viewed as asymmetric to the upside even by non-holders.
