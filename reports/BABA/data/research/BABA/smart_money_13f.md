## 3. SoftBank Stake History (Complete Exit)

### Timeline

| Date | Event | Shares | Stake | Gain |
|------|-------|--------|-------|------|
| 2000 | Initial $20M investment | ~34.4% at peak | Founding investor | - |
| 2014 (IPO) | BABA IPO at $68/ADS | ~32% | Largest shareholder | - |
| April 2020 | Prepaid forward contract initiated via Skybridge subsidiary | 512.3M ordinary shares (~64M ADSs) | ~20% of outstanding | - |
| Oct 2021 - Jan 2024 | Settlement of forward contracts | Gradual delivery | Declining to ~0% | - |
| April 2023 | Announced sale of majority of remaining stake (FT report) | Most of remaining | <5% | - |
| May 2024 | **Complete exit confirmed** | 0 shares | 0% | 1.26T yen ($8.5B), ~425x initial investment |
| Post-exit | SoftBank stated "no plans to buy or sell any new Alibaba shares" | - | - | - |

### Significance
- SoftBank's $20M investment in 2000 yielded ~$8.5B in gains over 24 years -- one of the most successful VC investments in history
- The complete exit removed the single largest overhang on BABA stock
- SoftBank used proceeds to fund AI investments (primarily ARM, other Vision Fund assets)
- **No longer a factor in BABA ownership analysis**

## 8. Overall Smart Money Assessment

### Bullish Signals
1. **Tepper maintains largest hedge fund position** ($1.15B, 15.6% of portfolio) -- trimming for risk management, not exiting
2. **Burry reversed to bullish calls** after brief bearish put phase -- $28M call option position
3. **Low short interest** (2% of float) -- limited institutional bearish conviction
4. **Insider buying** by Ma and Tsai at 2023 lows -- skin in the game
5. **$19.1B buyback remaining** -- management signaling undervaluation
6. **Daily Journal holding steady** post-Munger -- 195K shares maintained

### Bearish / Cautionary Signals
1. **SoftBank complete exit** -- removed largest historical bull (though motivated by portfolio rotation to AI, not BABA thesis)
2. **Druckenmiller exited Q4 2023** and has not re-entered -- one of the best macro traders avoiding China
3. **Only 45% institutional ownership** -- significantly below US mega-cap norms (70-80%), reflecting structural VIE/geopolitical discount
4. **Tepper trimming for 4+ quarters** -- reducing, not adding
5. **Burry's whipsaw** -- tactical, not conviction; could reverse again

### Net Assessment
Smart money positioning is **cautiously bullish**. The largest positions (Tepper, Daily Journal, ShawSpring) are holding with trims rather than exiting. New bullish entries (Burry calls) outweigh exits. The SoftBank overhang removal is a structural positive. However, the low institutional ownership base and absence of major new institutional accumulation suggest the market still applies a significant discount for China/VIE risk. The $19.1B buyback program provides a floor but is being executed at a moderate pace.
